How to calculate the true cost of an employee

  1. Use one employee and one consistent twelve-month budget. For a partial-year hire, build a matching partial-year cost and hours scenario rather than mixing annual salary with three months of hours.
  2. Enter gross salary, additional bonus and employer-only payroll contributions from your records. Do not add employee tax withholding to gross salary again.
  3. Add employer-paid benefits and a documented overhead allocation. Review software, equipment, training and office costs so each appears once.
  4. Enter one-time recruiting and onboarding costs separately. Use actual expected spending rather than an arbitrary salary multiplier.
  5. Enter paid hours and paid leave. Apply productive share to the remaining hours to allow for admin, meetings and non-delivery time.
  6. For a hiring recovery test, enter collected revenue and other variable costs per productive hour. Compare required hours with productive capacity and realistic demand.

Annual employee cost and productive-hour formulas

Recurring annual cost = salary + bonus + employer contributions + benefits + allocated overhead. First-year cost = recurring cost + one-time hiring cost.

Available hours = paid hours − paid leave hours. Productive hours = available hours × productive share ÷ 100. Recurring cost per paid hour = recurring cost ÷ paid hours. Recurring cost per productive hour = recurring cost ÷ productive hours.

First-year recovery hours = first-year cost ÷ (collected revenue per productive hour − other variable cost per productive hour). Other hourly cost excludes this same employee cost. A zero or negative contribution denominator has no finite recovery target.

Zero productive hours leaves productive-hour rates undefined. Zero salary leaves the cost-to-salary multiplier undefined. Neither condition turns the remaining employee costs into zero.

Employee cost example: paid hours versus billable hours

The example adds $60,000 salary, $3,000 bonus, $6,300 employer contributions, $9,000 benefits and $4,200 overhead: $82,500 recurring cost. The contribution amount is fictional, not a U.S. or UK statutory tax rate.

2,080 paid hours minus 240 leave hours leaves 1,840 available hours. At 75% productive share, 1,380 hours remain. Recurring cost is $39.66 per paid hour and $59.78 per productive hour. Adding $5,000 hiring cost produces $63.41 per first-year productive hour.

At $90 collected revenue and $10 other variable cost per productive hour, $80 is available to fund the hire. $87,500 ÷ $80 = 1,093.75 recovery hours. All 1,380 productive hours would leave $22,900 after the stated first-year cost, before other unentered commitments.

If productive share falls to 50%, capacity falls to 920 hours. The same first-year requirement still needs 1,093.75 recovery hours; annual contribution is $73,600 and the funding gap is $13,900. Lower utilization increases hourly cost even when salary is unchanged.

Use the result to decide whether the hire fits

  1. Compare the recurring result with next year’s budget and the first-year result with the hiring year. Recruiting costs should not silently repeat forever.
  2. Check whether forecast demand fills productive capacity. Available staff hours do not create customer work.
  3. Use the same cost boundary for a contractor comparison, including management time and delivery scope. This does not determine legal worker classification.
  4. Move payroll, employer contributions and benefit payments into the dated cash forecast. Annual affordability can still hide an earlier cash gap.

Direct labor budget: required hours are not a full annual hire cost

A direct labor cost budget starts with required activity times standard paid delivery hours times the relevant labor rate. For 200 fictional equivalent jobs, 1.5 paid delivery hours per job and a loaded rate of $32, the same-period labor budget is 300 hours and $9,600. A wage-only $24 rate would instead show $7,200 before the additional employer burden; do not call those two scopes the same cost.

This employee calculator answers a different but connected question: full annual employment cost and productive-hour recovery. Reconcile the period, paid-versus-productive hours and cost inclusions before taking its hourly result into a job budget. Do not also charge the same guaranteed salary as extra per-job labor.

Carry a consistent labor boundary into job costing

Use traced job hours and the appropriate labor rate; keep any employer burden out of the overhead pool if already included directly. A full annual employment cost and a short-period job cost answer different questions.

Assumptions and limits

This is one annual employee budget, not a payroll tax return or legal employment classification tool. Salary, bonus, employer contributions, benefits and allocated overhead are recurring costs. One-time hiring cost belongs only to first year. Paid leave already included in salary changes available hours, not the salary amount. Productive share applies after leave. Recovery assumes every modeled productive hour is delivered and collected at the entered net rate; it does not predict demand. It excludes unentered commitments and accounting adjustments.

Frequently asked questions

Does this calculate USA or UK payroll taxes?

No. Enter the employer-only annual amount from your current payroll records or adviser. Tax rates, thresholds and benefit rules vary by jurisdiction and date. Currency selection changes labels only.

Should I add paid holidays to salary?

Not when salary already pays those days. Subtract their hours from available time. Adding their salary cost again would double-count the same compensation.

What does fully loaded employee cost include?

Here it includes gross salary, extra bonus, employer contributions, benefits and allocated overhead. First-year cost also includes one-time hiring. The result only includes the amounts you enter.

Are productive hours the same as paid hours?

No. Paid hours include paid leave and may include administration. This tool removes paid leave, then applies your productive share to the remaining hours. For revenue recovery, use billable or deliverable hours.

What happens at zero productive hours?

Productive-hour costs are undefined. Annual employee cost remains and the contribution comparison shows the unfunded requirement.

Is the displayed funding surplus net profit?

No. It compares collected productive-hour contribution with this employee’s first-year cost. Other business commitments, interest, tax and accounting adjustments require a separate reconciliation.
