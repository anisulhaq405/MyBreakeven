Quick answer

Break-even ROAS is 1 divided by contribution margin before ads. An $80 order with $44 direct cost and a 3% fee leaves $33.60, or 42% contribution. Its break-even ROAS is 2.38×. At 4× ROAS on $1,000 spend, $680 remains after direct costs, fees and ads, before fixed overhead.

How to calculate break-even ROAS after fees

Use one campaign or a comparable group of orders over a consistent period. If you mix a low-margin product with a high-margin product, the average must represent the orders your advertising actually sells.

  1. Enter net average order value after discounts and refunds.
  2. Enter direct cost before advertising: products, fulfillment, shipping subsidies and flat fees.
  3. Enter the percentage selling fee separately. Do not count it again in direct cost.
  4. Add ad spend and ad-attributed net revenue for the same reporting window.
  5. Choose a target margin after ads and compare the actual return with both thresholds.

Break-even ROAS and maximum CPA formulas

Contribution per order = order value × (1 − fee rate) − direct cost. Contribution margin = contribution ÷ order value. Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ contribution margin.

Actual ROAS = attributed revenue ÷ ad spend. Contribution after ads = attributed revenue × contribution margin − ad spend. Maximum CPA before fixed overhead equals contribution per order.

Target ROAS = 1 ÷ (contribution margin − target margin). Use decimals in this formula. If the target margin is at least the pre-ad contribution margin, there is no finite ROAS that reaches that target with positive ad spend.

Does a 4× ROAS mean your ads are profitable?

These examples are fictional planning assumptions, not advertising benchmarks. At $80 order value, $44 direct cost and 3% fees, contribution is $80 − $2.40 − $44 = $33.60. Margin before ads is 42%; break-even ROAS is 1 ÷ 0.42 = 2.380952…×.

With $1,000 spend and $4,000 attributed net revenue, actual ROAS is 4×. Estimated contribution after ads is $4,000 × 0.42 − $1,000 = $680. A target of 10% revenue left after ads requires 1 ÷ (0.42 − 0.10) = 3.125×.

If direct cost rises to $60, contribution falls to $17.60, or 22%. Break-even ROAS rises to 4.54545…×. The same 4× return now leaves $4,000 × 0.22 − $1,000 = −$120. A fixed ROAS benchmark misses this cost change.

Read campaign contribution before increasing spend

A positive result covers the modeled direct costs, fees and ads. It still has to fund rent, salaries, software and owner pay. Use the ecommerce break-even calculator to add those monthly commitments.

Maximum CPA is a per-order ceiling before overhead, not a recommended bid. Actual platform attribution can include sales that would have happened without the ads. Compare the model with refund-adjusted records and your own testing.

Common ROAS calculation mistakes

  1. Using gross revenue before returns alongside net costs.
  2. Counting ads in direct order cost and again as ad spend.
  3. Comparing different attribution windows or currencies.
  4. Calling the contribution after ads net profit.
  5. Using one average margin when ads change the product mix.

BE ROAS versus a product gross-margin shortcut

BE ROAS, also written BEROAS, means break-even return on ad spend. Start with contribution before advertising when payment fees, shipping subsidy and other order-dependent costs must also be recovered. A product-only gross-margin shortcut can understate the required ad return.

In a separate fictional $100 order, product cost is $40, so the product-only gross margin is 60% and its reciprocal is 1.67×. Add $15 fulfillment and shipping, a $5 expected unrecovered return allowance and a $3 payment fee. Full pre-ad contribution becomes $37, or 37%, requiring 2.702702…× ROAS before fixed overhead. At 2× ROAS, attributed revenue of $2,000 against $1,000 spend leaves $2,000 × 0.37 − $1,000 = −$260 contribution after ads.

Use net revenue and a consistent return-cost definition so refunds or recovered inventory are not counted twice. This example assumes the stated $5 is additional unrecovered cost outside the already-defined net revenue. The calculator does not independently validate attribution, demand, repeat-customer value or your refund records.

Build a direct-cost ledger without counting refunds twice

The calculator uses retained net revenue: subtract discounts and refunded sales, and exclude sales tax or VAT collected for someone else. Use the same definition for average order value and the campaign revenue. The total-cost and itemized-cost methods are alternatives; selecting itemized costs replaces the total.

In the default fictional order, product cost 32, fulfillment and packaging 6, net merchant shipping 4, a flat transaction fee 0.50 and other unrecovered costs 1.50 sum to 44. A 3% selling fee on an 80 net order adds 2.40. Contribution before ads is 80 − 44 − 2.40 = 33.60. The numbers can represent any single currency; the selector does not convert them.

Refunded sales already removed from net revenue must not appear again as another full refund expense. Include only additional unrecovered handling, delivery, dispute fees or inventory losses in direct costs. Reconcile recovered stock and fee reversals. If your processor charges fees on gross receipts, derive an effective percentage of retained net revenue or an average flat cost, and enter it once.

Include agency costs and overhead at a stated ad budget

The order contribution threshold excludes fixed period costs. Expand the campaign controls to add a fixed support budget and the share of overhead the campaign must fund. These amounts use the same reporting period as ad spend. Keep percentage agency charges out of this fixed-cost assumption unless you calculate their amount for the entered budget and update it whenever spend changes.

With the default 42% contribution rate and 1,000 media spend, add 150 support cost and 250 allocated overhead. Total period outlay to recover is 1,400. Required net revenue = 1,400 ÷ 0.42 = 3,333.333…; required media ROAS = 3.333333…×. At 4,000 net revenue, the remaining amount is 4,000 × 0.42 − 1,000 − 150 − 250 = 280, or 7% of net revenue.

To leave a 10% margin after those same costs, required revenue is 1,400 ÷ (0.42 − 0.10) = 4,375; media ROAS is 4.375×. This differs from the contribution-only 3.125× target. The general formula is period target ROAS = (ad spend + support + allocated overhead) ÷ [ad spend × (contribution rate − target margin)]. It requires positive ad spend and a positive remaining contribution rate.

The 280 planning remainder is not automatically whole-store net profit. It excludes tax, unallocated commitments and unentered costs. Order counts inferred from revenue ÷ AOV are estimates, not measured new customers or evidence of incremental sales.

Translate ROAS into ACoS, a target CPA and a percentage

ROAS is attributed net revenue ÷ media spend. ACoS is media spend ÷ attributed net revenue × 100, so a 4× ROAS corresponds to 25% ACoS. This reciprocal relationship only compares the same spend and revenue basis. A total marketing-efficiency ratio that includes organic or returning-customer revenue answers a different question.

At the default 80 AOV and 33.60 contribution, the contribution-only CPA ceiling is 33.60. Keeping 10% of revenue means reserving 8 per order, leaving a target CPA of 25.60 and target ROAS of 80 ÷ 25.60 = 3.125×. That is 312.5% expressed as a ROAS percentage; it is not 3.125%. The CPA figure still excludes support and overhead unless those reserves are reflected in the chosen margin.

Google Ads describes target ROAS as an average conversion-value return and expresses it as a percentage. An economic threshold here is a planning constraint, not an instruction to set a bid strategy to that number. Check your historical performance, conversion lag and conversion-value setup; an overly high target can restrict traffic. If platform conversion values include tax or unadjusted refunds, first reconcile the basis instead of directly comparing unlike ROAS figures.

Use the next-spend decision, not a universal good-ROAS benchmark

The scenario table changes attributed revenue while holding media spend, order mix and period costs constant. It is a sensitivity check, not a forecast of demand. A 4× ROAS is profitable on contribution at a 42% margin but loses 120 on 1,000 spend at a 22% margin.

Before raising a budget, model the additional spend and additional retained revenue separately. For example, another 500 of spend that yields 1,000 net revenue is a 2× incremental ROAS. At 42% contribution it leaves 1,000 × 0.42 − 500 = −80 before any extra support cost, even if the earlier campaign average looked attractive.

Recheck discounts, product mix, shipping, refunded sales and conversion delays at a stated cutoff. Do not add revenue claimed by Meta, Google and another platform without deduplicating order IDs. Repeat purchase value belongs in a separately evidenced retention model; this calculator does not assume it. Set a CPA ceiling that also funds overhead

At the default $80 order value, $44 direct cost and 3% selling fee, pre-ad contribution is $33.60. The $33.60 contribution-only CPA ceiling leaves nothing for fixed overhead. In a separate planning case, reserve $8 per order for overhead and $4 for extra operating profit. This leaves $21.60 for acquisition and requires $80 ÷ $21.60 = 3.7037× ROAS, rounded.

The $12 remaining contribution is 15% of the $80 order value. Entering a 15% after-ad target represents that entire reserve under this example, not a claim that the calculator separately deducts actual rent and payroll. The allocation depends on achieved order volume; reconcile the real monthly costs before calling it net profit.

BE ROAS and break-even ROAS name the same contribution-threshold task here. Use retained net revenue, matching direct costs and one attribution window. A reported ROAS setting or a maximum CPA is not a guaranteed or recommended ad bid.

Assumptions and limits

Use retained net revenue, a stable average order mix and matching order costs, fees and attribution windows. Refunds must already be netted from revenue; enter only additional unrecovered costs once. Order contribution thresholds exclude fixed period costs. Period thresholds include only the support costs and overhead allocation you enter at the stated media budget; update those amounts when spending changes. Remaining contribution is before tax, unallocated commitments and unentered costs. No lifetime value, future demand, attribution accuracy or incrementality is assumed. Currency labels do not convert amounts.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good break-even ROAS?

There is no universal number. Your direct costs and fees determine the contribution margin. A 42% margin before ads needs about 2.38×; a 22% margin needs about 4.55×, both before overhead.

Is ROAS the same as ROI?

No. ROAS compares attributed revenue with ad spend. This tool also shows contribution after direct costs and ads, but does not calculate whole-business ROI.

Can I use this for Meta or Google Ads?

Yes, if you supply consistent net revenue and spend from the same reporting window. The tool does not connect to either platform or verify attribution.

Why is target ROAS not attainable?

Your requested post-ad margin leaves no positive allowance for ads. Lower the target or improve the order contribution before comparing returns.

Does maximum CPA include overhead?

No. It is the contribution available per order before advertising and fixed overhead. Set a lower operational ceiling if you need those orders to fund monthly commitments.

Can a profitable ROAS still leave the store losing money?

Yes. This calculator excludes monthly fixed overhead, owner pay and other unentered commitments. Campaign contribution must also cover those costs in the full store plan.

Can I calculate break-even ROAS before launching ads?

Yes. Enter a positive order value and the matching costs, then set ad spend and revenue to zero. The unit break-even ROAS and target CPA remain available. Actual ROAS and campaign ROAS including period costs need positive media spend.

How do I include fixed agency fees and overhead?

Expand the campaign controls and enter fixed support cost and allocated overhead for the same period. The period threshold includes these amounts at your stated media budget. Recalculate when the budget or fee amount changes; do not also include those costs in direct cost or media spend.

What if contribution is zero or my target margin is too high?

Zero or negative pre-ad contribution has no finite recovery ROAS for positive ad spend. If the target margin is at least the pre-ad contribution rate, no finite ROAS leaves that margin while paying for ads. Improve price or costs, or revise the target.

How do ROAS and ACoS relate?

With the same revenue and spend basis, ACoS percent equals 100 divided by ROAS. A 4× ROAS is 25% ACoS. Neither figure alone proves that fixed costs are covered.

Should I enter 3.125 or 312.5% in a ROAS percentage field?

A 3.125× multiple equals 312.5%. The calculator shows an economic threshold, not a recommended platform setting. Match conversion-value definitions and review historical performance before changing bidding targets.
