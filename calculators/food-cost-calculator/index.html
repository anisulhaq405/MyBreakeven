How to calculate food cost percentage from your records

  1. Choose inventory-count boundaries for a week, month or other completed period. Match purchases, transfers and net food sales to those dates and locations.
  2. Value opening and closing stock consistently at cost. Use invoices for goods received, then subtract supplier credits only if purchases are gross.
  3. Enter transfers across the counted boundary at cost. A move between two included storerooms is not a transfer in or out of this scope.
  4. If your reporting policy moves staff meals or complimentary food to another expense, enter only their documented ingredient cost. Otherwise leave these adjustments zero.
  5. Enter the theoretical cost of the actual sold menu mix, including modifiers, if known. Match the ingredient prices and scope used for the actual comparison.
  6. Use a waste log to explain part of the variance. Enter your own optional target, then review the signed gaps and source records before acting.

Food-cost formula: inventory bridge, adjustments and percentages

Net purchases = received purchases − supplier credits. Food available = opening inventory + net purchases + transfers in − transfers out. Gross food used = food available − closing inventory.

Adjusted food usage = gross food used − reclassified staff-meal cost − reclassified complimentary/promotional food cost. Adjusted food-cost percentage = adjusted usage ÷ net food sales × 100. Gross usage percentage is also shown so a reporting reclassification is not mistaken for reduced total consumption.

Actual–theoretical gap = adjusted usage − matched theoretical recipe cost. Gap in percentage points = gap ÷ net food sales × 100. Recorded waste is already in usage: remainder after recorded waste = gap − waste log cost. This signed remainder can include offsetting count, yield, price, portion or mix differences.

Target cost allowance = net food sales × your target percentage ÷ 100. Actual less allowance is a signed budget comparison, not a recoverable-savings promise. Sales at unchanged usage = adjusted usage ÷ (target ÷ 100), rounded up to cents. This holds total usage constant; it is an algebraic comparison, not a sales plan.

Checked examples with the same inventory and sales boundary

Default fictional period: $8,000 + $18,000 − $6,000 = $20,000 food used. Zero transfers and meal adjustments leave adjusted usage at $20,000. Divide by $60,000 net food sales for 33.3333…%, displayed as 33.33%. Food sales less adjusted ingredients is $40,000 before labor, other variable costs and overhead.

At $18,200 theoretical cost, the theoretical rate is 30.3333…%; the actual gap is $1,800 or 3.00 percentage points. A $600 recorded waste log leaves $1,200 to reconcile. Waste is not added to usage and is not subtracted when calculating food-cost percentage.

With a chosen 30% target, the allowance on $60,000 sales is $18,000. Actual usage is $2,000 above that allowance. Holding $20,000 usage constant requires $66,666.67 sales algebraically; additional sales normally change ingredient usage, so this is not a forecast.

Adjustment example: keep the original inventory and sales, add $500 transfers in and $300 transfers out, and reclassify $400 staff meals plus $200 complimentary food. Gross usage becomes $20,200 (33.67% of sales); adjusted usage is $19,600 (32.67%). The $600 reclassified cost remains elsewhere in business expenses.

Inventory check: if closing stock is $4,000 instead of $6,000 and other default inputs stay fixed, usage is $22,000 and food cost is 36.67%. An inaccurate closing count can therefore change the ratio without a real operating change.

Match each input with a supporting record

Inventory: dated physical counts, units and consistent valuation. Purchases/credits: receiving reports and supplier credit memos. Transfers: paired records at cost between locations or departments. Reclassified meals: authorized staff/comp logs and the receiving expense account. Sales: food-category POS net sales, with dates and discounts reconciled.

Theoretical usage: dishes and modifiers actually sold multiplied by standard recipe ingredient costs on a comparable valuation basis. A planned menu mix or a partial set of recipes is not a complete period benchmark. Waste: measured quantities priced at cost for this same period, excluding the meals already reclassified.

Supplier payment timing is separate from inventory receipt timing. Include food received on credit in purchases; exclude a deposit for food not yet received. Do not reduce net sales again for discounts already netted in the POS report.

Investigate a high food-cost percentage before changing prices

  1. Recheck high-value item counts, units, receiving cutoff, credit memos and transfers.
  2. Confirm food-only costs and food-only sales share one boundary. Adding unrelated beverage sales can make the rate look better without reducing usage.
  3. Review recipe prices, edible yield, actual portions, substitutions, modifiers and recorded waste. A variance alone does not identify theft or prove avoidable loss.
  4. Evaluate recipe contribution and full restaurant commitments separately. A lower food percentage does not establish profitability, and a meal reclassification does not create savings.

Assumptions and limits

All examples are hypothetical USD, not market benchmarks. Use ingredient costs for one matched inventory and sales scope. Transfers cross that scope; credit memos are subtracted once. Staff/promotional costs are optional documented expense reclassifications, not eliminated expenses. Waste remains in adjusted food usage. Theoretical cost must exclude the same reclassified meals and reflect the actual paid mix. Labor, packaging, platform fees and overhead are excluded. No accounting profit, demand forecast, automatic POS connection or guaranteed saving is calculated.

Frequently asked questions

What is the food-cost percentage formula?

For the adjusted scope, divide inventory-based food usage after documented transfers and meal reclassifications by matching net food sales, then multiply by 100. The inventory bridge stays visible alongside the adjusted ratio.

Is this a recipe cost calculator?

This page reconciles a completed inventory period. Use the separate recipe calculator for purchase packs, usable yield and cost per portion, then menu pricing to test complete variable costs.

What is a good food-cost percentage?

There is no universal target in this calculator. Choose a target from your menu mix, complete cost structure and budget. The illustrative 30% target is an assumption, not a national average or recommendation.

Should I subtract staff meals or complimentary meals?

Only when their ingredient cost is already in usage and your reporting policy reclassifies that cost to a separate expense. Enter cost, not menu price. Ordinary discounts do not justify subtracting ingredient cost; they already reduce net sales.

Should recorded waste be added to food used?

No. A physical closing count already reflects consumed, spoiled or discarded stock. The waste field explains part of the actual–theoretical gap; it does not change usage or the percentage.

How do supplier credits and transfers work?

Use gross received purchases and subtract credits once, or use net purchases with zero credits. Enter cross-boundary transfers at cost. Do not count a return both as a credit and as a transfer out.

Can the actual–theoretical gap be negative?

Yes. Actual usage can be below the recipe model, or logged waste can exceed the net gap because other favorable differences offset it. Check counts, valuation and portions; a negative amount is not automatically a verified saving.

Why are theoretical usage, waste and target optional?

Blank means unknown or unused; zero is a stated zero for theoretical usage and waste. A target must be positive. Missing optional fields leave the corresponding comparisons unavailable rather than inventing data.

Why can food cost exceed 100%?

Usage can exceed sales in a low-sales period or because of losses, timing, count errors or mismatched scopes. The tool keeps that result visible. Negative inventory usage is rejected for reconciliation.

Does sales less food cost mean profit?

No. It is the remainder before labor, packaging, payment/platform fees, occupancy and other expenses. Reclassified meals also remain a business expense. Use the linked restaurant plan for full costs.

What does sales at unchanged usage mean?

It divides the current total adjusted usage by your target rate. It assumes usage does not change; it is not a prediction of demand or a recommendation to raise prices.

Are currency amounts converted or saved?

Currency selection changes labels only. Enter every amount in the same currency. Inputs remain in this tab; CSV and print actions export the current scenario when you choose them.
