How to calculate financial ratios from your statements

  1. Choose the reporting entity and period. Enter all amounts in the same currency; use the period’s opening and closing balance sheets plus its income statement.
  2. Choose an individual measure in the direct-calculation selector to reveal exactly its required inputs. For a complete statement, work through liquidity, profitability, leverage, efficiency, cash flow, coverage and market-value input categories.
  3. Replace the fictional defaults or use Clear current inputs before starting. A blank input makes dependent measures unavailable; enter zero only when the source statement actually reports zero.
  4. Review the displayed formula and any reconciliation warnings. Enter a separate second dataset to compare periods, checking period length and consistent accounting policies first.
  5. Export the ratio results with their units, formulas and availability status. Compare your own trend and suitable industry evidence before interpreting strength or weakness.

Financial ratio formulas and their measurement bases

Liquidity is a balance-sheet snapshot. Current ratio = current assets/current liabilities. Quick ratio = (cash + eligible liquid investments + eligible trade receivables)/current liabilities. Cash ratio excludes receivables. Working capital = current assets − current liabilities; it is money, not a percentage or times ratio. The quick formula excludes inventory and prepayments rather than assuming all current assets are immediately spendable.

Gross margin = (revenue − COGS)/positive revenue × 100. Operating, net and EBITDA margins use their separately named profit numerator over the same revenue. Operating profit is not silently substituted for EBIT. EBITDA must be reconciled separately before entering it; it is not cash available for bills.

Closing liabilities/assets expresses the percentage of assets funded by all liabilities. Interest-bearing debt/equity uses loans and similar financing, while liabilities/equity also includes non-interest obligations. Interest coverage = period EBIT/positive interest expense. Zero interest is not displayed as an ordinary infinite coverage score.

Inventory turnover = period COGS/average inventory. Receivables turnover = period net credit sales/average net trade receivables. DSO = average trade receivables/net credit sales × actual period days. Asset turnover = period revenue/average assets. Average means (opening + closing)/2, not an assumed year-end balance.

ROA = period net income/average assets × 100. ROE = period net income/average equity × 100. These are period returns. A 90-day return is not automatically multiplied by four, and a two-point average can still misrepresent seasonal balance swings. Use a more representative average outside this tool when needed.

Understand all seven financial ratio categories

Liquidity ratios measure resources against current obligations; high inventory or overdue receivables can make apparent liquidity misleading.

Profitability ratios compare different profit definitions with revenue or invested capital. Do not confuse EBITDA with cash from operations, and match period flows to suitable balance averages.

Leverage and solvency ratios distinguish all liabilities from interest-bearing debt and opening equity from closing equity. Negative equity makes some conventional comparisons inappropriate.

Efficiency ratios use reported period sales, purchases and costs against opening/closing averages. The cash conversion cycle is inventory days plus receivables days minus payables days; DPO here uses purchases, not assumed COGS.

Cash-flow ratios use reported operating cash flow, not adjusted net profit. Free cash flow here subtracts capital expenditures, and a negative result may be meaningful.

Coverage ratios test ability to cover obligations under stated definitions. Cash-based debt service coverage is not automatically the DSCR covenant defined by a lender.

Market-value ratios need consistent valuation-date share prices, share counts or market capitalization. A private business without observable stock price should not invent a P/E or P/B.

Required versus undefined financial ratio results

Required means a source value is blank. Select that measure in the direct calculator and fill only its displayed input fields. Blank is unknown, not zero.

Undefined means the denominator is zero, negative or economically non-comparable. Do not turn undefined results into zero or infinity.

The starting example supplies just some statement balances. Optional share-market and cash-flow amounts are intentionally blank, so not all 55 results appear immediately. Currency choice changes labels without converting values.

Interpret results without a universal good-ratio score

Compare each result with the business model, seasonality, contract terms and the same measure over time. Inventory turnover differs between a grocer and an equipment distributor. Liquidity ratios do not reveal the exact dates on which customer receipts arrive and supplier payments fall due.

A second period can help identify a change, but it does not explain the cause. Percentage ratios show a percentage-point difference; a change from 10% to 12% is +2 percentage points, not +2% relative growth. Different-length periods make turnover and return deltas especially easy to misread.

Before concluding that leverage improved, reconcile the balance sheet and check whether liabilities were reclassified. Before concluding that margins improved, verify the revenue, expense recognition and one-off items. Use a cash-flow forecast for payment timing and a separate valuation method for business value.

Common financial ratio mistakes to avoid

Do not add liquid investments twice by entering the same balance in cash and investments. Do not replace missing net credit sales with total sales unless all sales are genuinely on credit. Do not divide annual revenue by an unrelated year-end asset value and call the result an average-assets turnover.

Do not mix consolidated assets with a subsidiary’s profit or use liabilities from one date and equity from another. The tool can warn when entered totals fail assets = liabilities + equity, but it cannot verify statements or infer omitted obligations.

Separate operating cash flow from EBITDA and working capital. The integrated leverage method selector changes the chosen definition, not your source balances. A denominator that is zero or economically non-comparable requires explanation rather than a fabricated zero result.

Assumptions and limits

Use one entity, currency and accounting policy for each dataset. Balance-sheet values are closing balances; profitability inputs are flows for the stated period. Average inventory, receivables, assets and equity use explicit opening and closing values. No opening value is inferred from a blank. Turnovers and returns describe the entered period, without automatic annualization. Equity multiplier and the two equity-based leverage measures use closing equity; return on equity uses average equity and is flagged when either endpoint is nonpositive. The ROE convention here is entered net income divided by average total equity; common-shareholder ROE needs an appropriately adjusted income/equity dataset. Liquid investments must be eligible short-term amounts not already in cash. Receivables must be collectible net trade balances. No accounting reconciliation, restatement, industry benchmark, valuation, forecast or credit eligibility decision is supplied automatically. A warning does not correct the source data. Results outside the supported range are withheld. Currency labels do not convert values.

Frequently asked questions

What financial ratios does this calculator calculate?

It provides liquidity, margins, leverage, coverage, turnover, DSO and returns: 55 measures including working capital as a money amount. Every measure shows its formula and input requirements.

Are financial ratios and equivalent ratios the same?

No. Financial ratios analyze business statements. Equivalent ratios are mathematical proportions such as 2:3 and 8:12; they are a separate task.

What is the financial leverage ratio formula?

There is more than one convention. The integrated selector distinguishes total assets/equity, total liabilities/equity and interest-bearing debt/equity using the same closing date.

Why are some results unavailable?

A required value may be blank or invalid, or the denominator may be zero or non-comparable. The result explains the missing requirement instead of assuming zero.

Does this annualize quarterly returns?

No. The entered period is displayed. Returns and turnover remain for that period, and DSO uses the explicitly entered number of days.

What is a good current ratio or debt ratio?

It depends on the business, industry, timing and trend. This tool does not apply a universal healthy range or lending approval score.

Can I compare two periods and export the results?

Yes. Enable the independent comparison dataset, enter its values and check period lengths. The CSV includes results, units, formulas and missing-input status.
