How to cost a recipe from the purchase pack

  1. Enter the purchase-pack cost, quantity and unit for each ingredient.
  2. Enter usable quantity used in the batch and its matching measurement type.
  3. Enter measured edible yield: 80% means one fifth of purchased quantity is unavailable. Do not add that trim loss again.
  4. Choose how many selling units the batch produces. One whole cake is one unit if the quote is for the entire cake.
  5. Add variable batch labor, per-unit packaging and other batch costs once. Set price and percentage fees to inspect contribution.
  6. Carry the itemized costs into menu pricing and review the monthly assumptions there.

Recipe cost, yield and contribution formulas

Usable purchased quantity = purchase quantity × unit conversion × yield rate. Ingredient cost = pack cost × compatible usable recipe quantity ÷ usable purchased quantity. Sum all ingredient lines for batch ingredient cost.

Ingredient cost per unit = batch ingredient cost ÷ selling units. Total variable cost per unit = ingredient cost + batch labor ÷ units + other batch costs ÷ units + per-unit packaging. Contribution = selling price × (1 − fee rate) − variable cost.

A zero selling price leaves percentage results undefined but does not stop recipe costing. Zero servings, zero yield, negative amounts and mixed measurement dimensions are rejected.

Checked recipe and whole-cake examples

Flour: $12 for 5 kg, with 500 g used, costs $1.20. Sugar: $4 for 1 kg, with 200 g used, costs $0.80. Butter: $6 for 500 g, with 100 g used, costs $1.20. With full yield, ingredients total $3.20.

The whole-cake preset uses $19.70 ingredients, $54 batch labor (three active hours at $18 per hour), $4 packaging and $5 other batch cost for one cake. Total variable cost is $82.70. At $130 and a 3% fee, contribution is $43.40; this is not net profit.

For a separate yield check, $24 for 5 lb with 80% usable yield gives 4 usable lb. Using 1.5 usable lb costs $9, or $0.75 across 12 servings.

Review portions and complete job costs

  1. Confirm recipe quantities describe usable ingredients rather than raw quantity that already includes trim.
  2. Check actual portions and ingredient price changes before quoting the same recipe again.
  3. For whole cakes, retain decoration time, packaging and delivery in the scope. Do not silently convert a whole-cake quote into slice revenue.
  4. Keep owner compensation once: either in variable labor or a separate monthly requirement for the same work.

Assumptions and limits

Recipe usage is usable edible quantity. Yield reduces the usable purchase quantity once. The cost equation can span multiple packs. Weight converts only to weight, volume only to volume, and count only to count. Ingredients must use compatible units; cups and mass-to-volume conversions are outside this model. Batch labor and other costs allocate once to selling units. Contribution excludes monthly fixed costs, unentered costs and taxes.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use pounds and grams together?

Yes, both are weight units. The tool converts them to a consistent base before costing. It rejects a weight purchase paired with volume usage because no density is assumed. US fl oz means the US customary volume unit, not Imperial or a rounded nutrition-label measure; use the metric quantity when that is the pack measurement.

Does recipe cost include labor and overhead?

Ingredient cost is shown separately. Enter variable batch labor, packaging and other batch costs to see contribution. Monthly overhead belongs in the menu or whole-business funding plan.

Can I price a custom cake?

Use the whole-cake preset and one selling unit for a whole-cake quote. Replace ingredients, labor and delivery with the accepted scope, then test monthly funding and capacity in menu pricing.

How does yield affect cost?

Yield reduces purchased usable quantity. A lower yield makes each usable quantity more expensive. Do not add the same trim or waste again elsewhere.

Are costs saved to my account?

No. Inputs remain in this tab. The optional menu transfer stores only a one-time cost summary in session storage and does not submit it to an account.

Does changing the unit dropdown change its quantity?

No. Select the correct unit for the quantity you enter. The cost calculation converts the declared units; changing a label does not rewrite the input quantity.
