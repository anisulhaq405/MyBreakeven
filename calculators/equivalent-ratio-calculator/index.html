Quick answer

Business ratios are meaningful only when the numerator, denominator, measurement units and time periods are consistent. Compare cost divided by revenue, debt divided by equity, labor hours per unit, or product revenue shares. Equal ratios can occur at different business scales and do not establish equal profits.

Business ratio formulas and what they measure

Cost-to-revenue ratio = eligible costs ÷ matching revenue × 100. If costs are $30,000 and revenue $100,000, the ratio is 30%. A second period with $45,000 costs and $150,000 revenue is also 30%, even though the business is larger. This is not the same as net profit margin.

Debt-to-equity = interest-bearing debt ÷ equity when those are the selected consistent definitions. $40,000 debt divided by $60,000 equity equals 0.667×. Interpret the figure alongside cash flows, debt service, maturity dates and industry context; lower is not automatically better in every capital structure.

Labor intensity = labor hours ÷ finished units. Twenty hours for 100 units equals 0.20 hours per unit; thirty hours for 100 units equals 0.30 hours per unit. The increase is 0.10 hour per unit and may warrant checking staffing, downtime, quality or product mix.

Revenue mix share = product revenue ÷ total selected product revenue × 100. Track each product as a percentage of the same basket and compare percentage-point changes, not just absolute sales.

Equal ratios do not imply equal total sales, absolute expenses, cash balances or business value. Always examine monetary changes alongside normalized KPIs.

A target is a planning threshold selected by the user. The tool offers areas to investigate, not a diagnosis that any one change caused the outcome.

Business scaling, benchmarking and revenue-mix tables

Proportional scaling is useful for comparing periods of different sizes. A $30 cost for $100 revenue and $45 cost for $150 revenue both represent 30 cents of cost per revenue dollar. This normalization does not identify cost categories or estimate future spending without assumptions.

In a three-product revenue mix, calculate every product share against the same selected product total. A product can gain sales dollars while its share declines because other products grew faster. Review contribution margins before declaring a mix shift beneficial.

Compare ratios only across compatible units, matching accounting policies, the same period length and the same business model where possible. A numeric threshold without those checks can create false benchmarks.

Common mistakes in business ratio interpretation

Do not interpret an unchanged ratio as unchanged financial performance. Absolute revenue, cost, cash flow, capital employed and risk can change substantially.

Never confuse debt-to-equity with debt-to-assets, cost-to-revenue with gross margin, or labor hours per unit with units per labor hour. Their numerators and denominators differ.

Treat targets as internal planning choices rather than universal industry standards. Check data quality, seasonality, product mix, capacity and definitions before deciding on operational changes.

Use Advanced ratio mathematics only if you need exact proportional equations, missing-value solving or scaled ratios. Those mathematical outcomes do not substitute for a financial statement analysis.

Assumptions and limits

Business inputs should be nonnegative and use consistent reporting periods, units and numerator/denominator definitions. The denominator must be positive. For labor hours per unit, product quality and product mix must be comparable. Debt-to-equity here is debt divided by equity, not a credit recommendation. The target is user-defined, not an industry benchmark. The exact-ratio engine supports fractions, decimals, two- and three-term vectors and solving x in advanced mode, but mathematical equivalence alone does not measure profitability.

Frequently asked questions

What does this business ratio calculator compare?

It compares cost-to-revenue, debt-to-equity, labor hours per output unit and three-product revenue mix between two periods or businesses.

How is cost-to-revenue calculated?

Divide selected costs by matching revenue and multiply by 100. A result of 30% means 30 cents of selected cost per dollar of revenue.

What does an unchanged ratio mean?

The normalized proportion is unchanged. Total revenue, expense, profit and risk can still change.

What is a percentage-point difference?

It is the direct subtraction of percentage values: moving from 30% to 36% is an increase of 6 percentage points.

How does the target versus actual comparison work?

Enter a business-chosen cost, debt or labor intensity target. The tool compares it with Period B and shows the numerical gap; it is not an industry benchmark.

Can I compare product revenue mix?

Yes. Enter three product revenue amounts for each period. The calculator shows each product share and its change in percentage points.

Does a lower debt-to-equity ratio always mean a healthier company?

No. Capital structure, cash flow, borrowing costs, maturity and equity quality also matter.

Can I still solve equivalent ratios with fractions?

Yes. Switch to Advanced ratio mathematics to compare ratios, solve x and generate exact scaled proportions.
