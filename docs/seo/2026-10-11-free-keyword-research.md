# MyBreakeven: free keyword research and implementation plan

Research date: 11 October 2026. Target market: United States, English. Website: MyBreakeven.com. No paid subscription, paid trial, extension installation or advertising campaign was started.

## Decision

Improve existing calculator and article pages first. Most verified relevant intents already have an owner among the 133 articles inspected in the local content inventory. Do not publish another restaurant-budget, labor-percentage or ROAS-formula article simply to repeat a keyword. A cleaning monthly-budget resource is a possible new deliverable because the existing cleaning worksheet costs one job, rather than a whole month.

This is a focused keyword and content plan, not certification that all 133 articles are fully optimized. Current live indexing and fresh complete Search Console query-to-page data were not re-audited in this research run.

## Evidence and confidence

The numeric observations below came from live free reports, with United States selected, on the research date. Semrush reports are separate from WordStream reports. They are provider estimates of monthly searches, not exact Google counts or forecasts of visits. The default reporting periods were not independently verified for each report. Close variants may share demand; never sum them to claim total available traffic.

Semrush KD is an organic difficulty estimate. WordStream's Competition column is advertising competition, not organic keyword difficulty. Neither determines whether our site can rank.

One cross-check illustrates uncertainty: “break even calculator” was 720 in Semrush and 880 in WordStream. Keep both values and their sources; do not average them or present either as exact.

## Implementation queue

### 1. Restaurant budget: improve the existing asset

Keep /blogs/restaurant-budgeting/. The current article already includes a monthly budget, actual-versus-budget work, FAQs and an Excel download at /downloads/restaurant-monthly-budget-template.xlsx. The downloadable format was checked in the content inventory and local public assets.

Proposed title/H1: **Restaurant Budget Template: Monthly Plan & Example**

Proposed meta description: **Download an editable restaurant budget template for Excel. Plan sales, food costs, loaded payroll and overhead, then compare budget with actual results.**

Opening: identify it as a monthly restaurant budget template, explain its assumptions and put the download near the relevant worked example.

Download-section H2: **Restaurant budget template for Excel**.

Useful FAQ: **How do I use a restaurant budget template?** Give practical worksheet instructions and explain budget versus actuals. Add or revise only if existing text does not already answer it.

Contextual product link: /calculators/restaurant-break-even-calculator/, anchor **restaurant break-even calculator** after the budget contribution example. Retain the existing labor guide link. No duplicate new article and no URL change.

### 2. ROAS: strengthen the product-to-guide connection

Keep /calculators/break-even-roas-calculator/ as the interactive-intent owner and /blogs/break-even-roas-formula/ as the explanatory-intent owner.

Candidate product title: **Break-Even ROAS Calculator: Margin, Fees & CPA**

Candidate meta: **Calculate break-even ROAS and maximum CPA from your margin and order costs. Test fees and returns, and see why the ad threshold differs from business profit.**

Review existing metadata first; preserve it if it already describes these features clearly. Use the primary calculator phrase naturally in H1 and opening. Keep the formula explanation below the working tool, with a worked example and boundary of costs included.

For the formula guide, retain its already relevant primary title. Add a contextual link to the calculator after the calculation only if that link is missing; the inspected article already has a calculator link. Product page should link back to the formula guide where it explains assumptions.

FAQ topics: **Does break-even ROAS cover fixed overhead?** and **Should returns be included?** Existing answers cover these issues; improve clarity only where needed. Do not duplicate an already adequate FAQ.

### 3. Restaurant labor: retain the strong intent match

Keep /blogs/restaurant-labor-cost-percentage/. Its current title already targets the exact keyword and its description covers loaded payroll. Do not rewrite simply because research repeated the same term.

Suggested formula heading where needed: **How to calculate restaurant labor cost percentage**. Explain payroll cost boundary, comparable sales denominator and a checked example. State treatment of taxes, benefits and owner work.

Link to the existing budget article for planning and the restaurant calculator for testing the monthly impact. Do not invent a universal “good percentage” or strip payroll costs to make a target look better.

### 4. Cleaning: one genuinely different resource

Keep /blogs/cleaning-business-monthly-expenses/ for the 40/month expenses intent. Its current job-based budget and payroll reconciliation are useful; add “cleaning company expenses” only in natural relevant prose.

Proposed new resource: **Cleaning Business Budget Template: Monthly Plan**. Use “janitorial budget template” in a commercial-cleaning section and a relevant visible FAQ.

Build an actual downloadable monthly template before promising one: revenue/bookings, productive and paid hours, payroll, supplies, transport, fixed costs, budget versus actual and capacity assumptions. Include a filled, explicitly hypothetical example and blank input version. The current one-job worksheet does not satisfy this monthly-template promise.

This resource is a modest, high-fit experiment supported by 30/month Semrush demand, not a large traffic opportunity. Link it from the monthly-expenses guide and back to /calculators/cleaning-business-break-even-calculator/. Choose the final resource URL through existing routing conventions before publication.

### 5. General formula and calculation guides

Give the existing formula guide units and revenue formula sections, clearly labeled variables and one checked example. Preserve the separate how-to guide's step-by-step intent. Link explanations to the main general calculator and link the calculator back to the relevant explanatory guide.

High volume does not mean easy opportunity: Semrush's how-to query KD was 49 and established publishers appear in the calculator results. Before changing multiple generic pages, use Search Console query-to-page data to see which URL already receives impressions. Do not put every formula variant into every title.

### 6. Remaining industries: small targeted improvements

Landscaping and salon already have exact-intent profit-margin guides. Keep their relevant titles; improve owner-pay boundaries, arithmetic and links to their industry calculators before adding articles.

Agency: current billable-utilization guide answers the 20/month intent. Candidate shorter title: **Agency Utilization Rate: Billable Hours & Capacity**. Clearly explain the hour denominator and paid time; preserve meaningful content rather than filling a low-volume page with variants.

Photography: candidate title **Photography Pricing Packages: Costs, Time & Profit**. Preserve the owner perspective, package cost table, editing time and contribution example. Explain hypothetical sample prices; do not present them as verified market rates.

Detailing: retain the broader existing car-detailing-price-list guide. A mobile subsection can explain travel time, setup and route costs. A separate mobile-pricing article is not justified by this mixed-intent estimate alone; first inspect Search Console overlap with the existing travel-fee and maintenance-plan articles.

For these clusters, use the calculator URLs already linked by the respective articles. Validate routes instead of inventing new product URLs.

## Placement and verification rules

One primary intent per URL. Put its main phrase in a concise title and relevant H1 when it accurately describes the page. Use supporting phrases in sections that answer them. Meta descriptions should explain the page and its useful output; they are not a list of variants.

FAQs should resolve real questions visible to readers. Google states that FAQ rich results stopped appearing from 7 May 2026. Do not sell FAQ schema as a rich-result or ranking guarantee.

Keep existing relevant URLs. Use one industry calculator link at a useful decision point and a few directly relevant article links; do not add every product link to every page.

Before publishing: verify template download, title and description rendered in the page, canonical URL, indexability, calculator routes, visible FAQ/schema consistency and every worked example. Record the publication date and specific edits.

Measurement: record current US Search Console impressions, clicks, CTR and position by query and owner URL before the next edits. Compare equal 28-day windows after indexing; use a later window if impressions are too small. Track calculator starts and useful actions in GA4 separately from search estimates. Do not repeatedly rewrite recently improved restaurant pages while awaiting enough data.
