Calculation and decision controls

Separate the balance-sheet measure from the seasonal funding gap

Net working capital is current assets minus current liabilities. That balance-sheet measure is useful, but it does not say whether Friday’s payroll clears before Monday’s customer payment. A profitable landscaping job can require temporary funding if plants, soil, fuel and labor are paid first.

Start the cash schedule with reconciled, available business cash. List each receipt by its expected clearing date and each payment by its actual due date. Include equipment purchases, debt payments and owner withdrawals in separate lines when relevant. Those lines affect funding even though they do not all belong in operating profit. Do not count an unapproved credit limit or a customer promise as cleared cash.

Week one includes $6,000 materials, $3,000 payroll and $1,000 other payments. Week two includes $4,000 materials, $5,000 payroll and $2,000 other payments. Total receipts are $28,000 and total payments $36,000, so $12,000 + $28,000 − $36,000 = $4,000 closing cash. The lowest weekly closing balance is −$3,000. A negative forecast is a funding warning, not an instruction to let a bank account overdraw.

If management chooses a $5,000 minimum cash floor, the additional cash needed at the lowest weekly close is $5,000 − (−$3,000) = $8,000. The floor is a choice for this case, not a landscaping-industry standard. Check daily order within each week: a Monday payroll before a Friday receipt can create a deeper trough.
