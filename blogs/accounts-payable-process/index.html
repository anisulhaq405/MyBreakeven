Accounts Payable Process: Approve, Pay and Reconcile

Quick answer

The accounts payable process verifies supplier obligations, records approved invoices and credits, schedules authorized payments and reconciles the remaining balance. Match what was ordered, received and billed before paying, and preserve evidence for exceptions.

Read the source-backed application and limits. Worked business amounts are fictional teaching examples.

All amounts and operating results below are original fictional teaching examples in USD. They are not customer results, industry averages or forecasts. Use your own dated records and applicable accounting policies.

Capture the invoice and validate the supplier record

Use a controlled intake location and record supplier, invoice number, currency, date, terms, purchase reference and total. Check duplicates using more than a file name: the same bill can arrive as a PDF, reminder and statement. A supplier statement summarizes activity; it is not automatically a new expense.

Separate supplier-detail changes, invoice approval and payment release where practical. Verify a requested bank-detail change through a previously trusted contact route, not only the new email. In a very small business, an independent owner review of the evidence can compensate for limited staffing, but it should be an actual recorded check.

Match receipt and price, then resolve exceptions

Microsoft’s invoice-matching documentation distinguishes price comparison with the purchase order from the additional receipt-quantity check. Choose controls that fit the purchase. A service may need approved completion evidence instead of a warehouse receipt; a non-PO bill needs a documented authorization route, not automatic approval.

For an independent goods example, a purchase order requests 100 components at $20, but the receiving record shows only 90 accepted. A supplier invoice for $2,000 raises a $200 quantity exception. Investigate the missing ten and request correction or evidence before approval. Do not silently change the receipt to 100 to make the documents agree. Recognition of goods received and treatment of any dispute still follow the accounting policy.

The ledger closes at $9,000 invoices − $2,500 allocated payments − $300 approved credit = $6,200. V-203’s remaining $1,000 is twelve days overdue on November 6. The credit on V-202 is not a payment; retain the supplier reference and reason. A dispute flag is not permission to delete a supported obligation.

The separate month movement is $4,500 opening AP + $4,500 new bills − $2,500 payments − $300 credits = $6,200. Compare that result with the supplier-detail total and GL control balance. Do not add the opening balances to a closing ledger that already includes them.

Authorize the payment without duplicating the expense

Prepare a proposed payment run with invoice references, due dates, available cash and hold reasons. Review agreed discounts against actual terms; do not assume delaying every payment improves the business. A planned payment is not a completed bank transaction. Retain release approval and settlement evidence, then allocate the payment to the correct invoice.

If $1,500 of V-201 is paid after that invoice was already recorded, reduce cash and the payable. Posting another $1,500 expense at payment would duplicate the cost. A purchase can instead relate to inventory, equipment or a prepayment, so the AP workflow must not force every bill into operating expense.

Close with supplier, ledger and cutoff checks

Compare supplier statements with internal invoices, payments and credits. Investigate missing invoices, unallocated payments, duplicates and payments in transit. Reconcile supplier detail to the AP control account at the same cutoff. Review received goods and completed services not yet invoiced, keeping necessary accruals separate until matched so they are not counted twice.

Use liability classification for the reporting boundary and the customer collection workflow for incoming amounts. The AR-versus-AP comparison explains the distinction. Feed actual supplier due dates into the cash plan; a reconciled $6,200 balance does not itself say which payments are due tomorrow.

Keep matching tolerance and payment authority separate

Microsoft’s matching documentation distinguishes two-way price checks from three-way checks that also compare receipt quantities. In the original case, ninety accepted units at $20 support $1,800 of received goods, while the $2,000 invoice contains a $200 quantity exception. A system tolerance is a configured review threshold, not proof that an incorrect invoice is economically correct or that missing goods arrived.

Retain the original invoice, receiving record, exception decision and supplier correction. SAP’s reconciliation example illustrates keeping the original document and the reconciliation trail separately. A documented partial payment, credit or corrected invoice must explain its effect on the remaining supplier balance. Resolving a matching icon does not itself show that a bank payment was authorized or settled; those events need their own references.

Protect supplier-master changes as a separate control. The FBI’s IC3 guidance recommends verifying account-change requests through another channel. For an actual payment run, confirm changed bank details through a previously trusted contact route, retain the verification and review the payee independently of invoice approval. If only one person operates the business, keep a visible callback and approval record rather than treating the sender’s email as confirmation. After settlement, allocate the receipt or payment reference to the invoice, reconcile the resulting $6,200 example balance, and investigate any unexplained supplier-statement difference before closing the period.

Frequently asked questions

Is a supplier statement another bill?

Not automatically. Match its lines to existing invoices, credits and payments before posting anything new.

Does invoice approval prove payment occurred?

No. Payment release and settlement require their own evidence.

Should paying a recorded bill create another expense?

No. Normally it settles the recorded payable; confirm the original posting and accounting policy.

Can a non-PO invoice skip controls?

It needs an appropriate documented authorization and evidence route for the purchase.

Does an allowed matching tolerance prove the invoice is correct?

No. It is a configured control threshold. The underlying purchase, receipt and supplier evidence still determine the supported amount.

Does invoice approval also approve a changed bank account?

Treat bank-detail verification as a separate control using a trusted independent contact route.
