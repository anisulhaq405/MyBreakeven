Quick answer

A business proposal explains a specific client problem, the deliverables you will provide, the schedule, price, assumptions and process for acceptance. Build the delivery cost case before promising the scope, then turn the agreed details into the appropriate written engagement.

Read the source-backed application and limits. Worked business amounts are fictional teaching examples.

All business names, amounts and operating results in the worked examples are fictional teaching assumptions in USD. They are not industry averages, actual customer results or forecasts for your business.

A proposal can answer a request for proposals or start a conversation with a potential buyer. In either case, adapt it to the decision the client must make. A business plan explains the venture to owners or funders; a client proposal offers a defined engagement. Do not substitute a generic company history for an answer to the client’s requirement.

Check a fictional fixed-fee project before quoting

A catalog photography proposal includes 20 finished images, one agreed background treatment and one consolidated correction round within the stated scope. The seller estimates 20 production hours plus ten planning, communication and editing hours. At an internal loaded rate of $40, included labor is $1,200. Add $300 studio and materials and $150 travel for a defined delivery cost of $1,650.

At a $2,400 fee, project surplus after those included costs is $750, or 31.25% of the fee. Markup on that same $1,650 cost is 45.45%. The percentages use different denominators. Neither establishes net company profit because shared overhead and other omitted costs still need coverage. The $40 rate must represent the chosen cost basis consistently.

Show how one scope change affects the quote

Ten extra hours at the same internal rate add $400. If the fee stays $2,400, included delivery cost becomes $2,050 and project surplus falls to $350, or 14.58%. If the client also needs new props, travel or a new deadline, the cost change may be larger. An undefined revision promise makes the original estimate unreliable.

State which customer inputs are required, how feedback is consolidated, what counts as an accepted output and how requested changes are evaluated. Record the proposed impact on scope, delivery dates and fee before commencing additional work. This teaching template does not establish enforceable contract terms for every jurisdiction.

Connect milestones to actual cash requirements

An illustrative 40% / 40% / 20% payment schedule splits the $2,400 fee into $960, $960 and $480. The schedule must be negotiated, documented and matched to the work and invoice dates. A milestone described in a proposal is not a cleared payment. Test the cash schedule if the second installment arrives late.

Do not promise the client an unsupported revenue increase to justify the price. Use measurable deliverable acceptance or explicitly labeled scenarios instead. Taxes, intellectual property, confidentiality, cancellation and liability depend on the actual engagement; resolve those terms with suitable review rather than treating this example as a complete legal agreement.

Complete and review the proposal before sending

  1. Replace the sample client, objective, deliverables and assumptions.
  2. Recompute all hours, costs, optional items and the total fee.
  3. Check the planned completion date against practical capacity and client dependencies.
  4. Reconcile milestone amounts to the total and record the due-date basis.
  5. Review commercial and legal terms for the actual engagement and retain the accepted version.

Turn deliverables and changes into an acceptance record

Salesforce’s proposal guide supports organizing client-specific scope, timing, price and acceptance. It is vendor education, not legal authority. For the 20-image case, identify the product list, final file format, dimensions, agreed background and the person authorized to approve delivery. Write observable completion criteria instead of promising an undefined “professional result.”

Record which products, brand files and approvals the client must supply before production can proceed. Explain how a delayed input changes the proposed schedule. Give an additional image or correction request its own reference, estimated extra hours, impact on delivery and proposed fee decision. Confirm that decision before starting the additional scope. A recorded request alone is not evidence that the customer accepted a changed fee. Keep the accepted version and its supporting correspondence together so subsequent invoices can be traced to the same scope.

The $40 internal loaded rate is an estimating assumption. It can include costs that do not vary with one additional project, so the $750 and $350 differences are labeled project surplus after included costs rather than automatically treated as textbook variable-cost contribution. Check which underlying salaries or shared costs are already in the rate before adding overhead. The $960, $960 and $480 installments sum to the $2,400 fee, but due dates are not receipt dates. Map cash timing independently, including a delayed second installment. Review intellectual property, cancellation, liability and other engagement-specific terms with appropriate expertise rather than treating the worksheet as a complete enforceable agreement.

Frequently asked questions

What should a business proposal include?

A client objective, defined deliverables, scope boundaries, schedule, price, assumptions, payment basis and an acceptance process suited to the engagement.

Is the project surplus percentage the same as markup?

No. Here project surplus divided by fee and project surplus divided by included cost use different denominators. Standard contribution margin uses variable costs; this example instead uses its stated loaded project-cost boundary.

Can this worksheet replace a contract?

It is a teaching and drafting record. Whether additional terms or a separate agreement are needed depends on the actual engagement and applicable law.

How should I handle extra revisions?

Define the original revision scope, then review and document the effect of additional work on cost, price and delivery dates before proceeding.

Is the $750 example surplus the company’s net profit?

No. It is the fee less the specified project costs. Shared overhead, financing, tax and any omitted costs still need consideration; the internal loaded-rate basis also matters.

Does a customer request automatically approve an extra fee?

No. Record the requested change, proposed scope/date/fee effect and the actual acceptance before beginning additional work.
