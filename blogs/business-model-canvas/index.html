Quick answer

A Business Model Canvas summarizes how one business model serves customers and earns revenue through nine connected blocks. Its value comes from testing the relationships between those blocks, including whether the proposed price, costs and delivery capacity can work together.

Read the source-backed application and limits. Worked business amounts are fictional teaching examples.

All business names, amounts and operating results in the worked examples are fictional teaching assumptions in USD. They are not industry averages, actual customer results or forecasts for your business.

Connect the customer promise to the operating numbers

Assume ten office customers each pay $600 a month: revenue is $6,000. Each requires eight worker-hours at an assumed job-varying loaded delivery cost of $30 per hour, so delivery labor is $2,400. Supplies are $30 per customer, or $300. Contribution after those included delivery costs is $3,300. Subtract $1,800 defined monthly overhead for an illustrative $1,500 operating result before omitted financing and tax items.

Ten customers need 80 worker-hours. If practical delivery capacity is 96 hours, the same eight-hour scope supports at most 12 whole customers, before considering route and scheduling constraints. A promise of unlimited additional service contradicts the cost and capacity blocks. Do not count the same salaried labor in both the $30 hourly rate and the $1,800 overhead.

Change a block and follow the consequences

Suppose the value proposition changes to include two extra worker-hours per customer at the same fee. Ten customers now require 100 hours, exceeding the stated 96-hour capacity. At the same $30 cost rate, delivery labor becomes $3,000, contribution $2,700 and the result after unchanged overhead $900. That arithmetic does not make the schedule feasible: an actual capacity response may add further cost.

The choice could require a narrower scope, a justified price change, a different customer segment or additional resources. Update channels and relationship expectations as well as the spreadsheet. A model canvas should expose those dependencies rather than hide them behind separate attractive statements.

Rank assumptions by consequence and missing evidence

Mark every statement as observed, supported by a limited test or still assumed. Customer willingness to pay, delivery time and repeat purchase deserve early attention because they can change several blocks at once. Write a test with a customer group, offer, cost ceiling, measured outcome and decision date.

For the cleaning case, time a fully scoped pilot including travel and inspection, then obtain a separate repeat-service decision. An enthusiastic survey response is not equivalent to paid recurring demand. Archive the original canvas and change only the blocks the evidence affects; record why each version differs.

Use the right next document for the decision

A canvas describes the model; a feasibility study examines whether it should proceed under actual constraints. A business plan organizes the chosen venture’s operating and funding story. A sales forecast translates demand assumptions into a dated volume schedule. None of those documents independently proves a profitable business.

Continue with the business plan template, the customer profitability method for cost-to-serve differences or the sales forecast for demand and capacity. Keep the canvas focused on one coherent model so its assumptions can be challenged together.

The feasibility study tests whether the model can proceed; strategic planning records which customers and resource priorities the business will choose.

Trace each changed promise through the connected blocks

The official Strategyzer canvas guidance treats the model as connected hypotheses that should be dated and revised as evidence changes. Put one accountable owner and version date on the record. Attach a short evidence reference to important assumptions rather than hiding every uncertainty in one general notes field.

In the original cleaning case, expanding from eight to ten worker-hours per customer changes the resources, activities and cost blocks immediately. Ten customers would require 100 hours against 96 available. The $900 result after unchanged overhead does not resolve that four-hour operational shortage. A new distant segment can also change travel, channels, the support relationship and the acceptable price. Do not revise only the revenue block while leaving the delivery system unchanged. State what actual change supplies the missing capacity and recheck its cost before committing to the promise.

This numerical example assumes the $30 loaded delivery rate varies with the included worker-hours and that its underlying labor is not also counted in overhead. A real salaried employee’s allocated rate may be useful for estimating full service cost without being an avoidable payment for one more customer. Mark that distinction before using the amount as variable-cost contribution or a cash forecast input. A completed canvas describes the proposed model; paid delivery, customer acceptance and repeat decisions test different parts of it. Archive the original and explain each changed block. Frequently asked questions

Is a Business Model Canvas the same as a business plan?

No. The canvas summarizes one model and its assumptions. A plan develops the chosen operating and funding approach in more detail.

Do all nine blocks have to be certain before drafting?

No. Label assumptions and test important unknowns. Filling every block does not validate the model.

Can revenue streams alone demonstrate profitability?

No. Reconcile delivery costs, shared commitments and practical capacity. Also check when customer cash arrives.

Is the downloadable worksheet an official canvas?

No. It is an original MyBreakEven case and evidence record. The article links to Strategyzer for the official tool and terms.

Does a completed canvas prove the business is feasible?

No. It records connected hypotheses. Customer demand, delivery capacity, costs and funding still require evidence and their own decision review.

Is an internal hourly labor rate always a variable cash payment?

No. A loaded or allocated rate can include committed salary or shared costs. Identify the actual cost behavior and avoid counting its underlying amount again in overhead.
