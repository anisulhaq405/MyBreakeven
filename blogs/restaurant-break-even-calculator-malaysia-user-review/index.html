Assume 26 open days. The owner allowance is included separately only because it is absent from fixed commitments. Classify committed payroll with fixed costs and variable labor with orders; never charge the same work twice.

Operating break-even: RM18,000 ÷ RM15 = 1,200 orders, or RM36,000 revenue. Including the separate owner allowance requires 1,400 orders. Funding both that allowance and RM6,000 additional profit requires RM27,000 ÷ RM15 = 1,800 orders, or RM54,000 revenue.

The 1,800-order target averages 69.23 orders over 26 days. 70 orders per open day is a rounded planning pace, not an assertion that every service has equal demand. At 1,560 orders (60 per open day), contribution would be RM23,400: RM2,400 remains after the included overhead and owner allowance, leaving the RM6,000 target short by RM3,600.

For the general method, use our restaurant break-even guide. For table-service counts, use the covers-per-night guide. One order can cover several guests; do not label an order target “customers” without an explicit conversion.

Discount pressure: a 10% price reduction lowers price to RM27. The 2% fee falls to RM0.54, but ingredients and other per-order costs remain unchanged. Contribution becomes RM12.06. Higher sales volume is useful only if incremental contribution and capacity support the promotion.

Delivery pressure: at RM30, a 25% combined percentage fee is RM7.50. With an extra RM2 packaging cost, contribution is RM6.10. Actual contracts can use different fee bases, subsidies, taxes or fixed charges; enter those terms rather than copying this example.

Menu and channel mix: with 70% base orders and 30% delivery orders, weighted contribution is (0.70 × RM15) + (0.30 × RM6.10) = RM12.33. The resulting 2,190-order target assumes that mix stays stable. A best-selling menu item cannot represent the whole business if lower-contribution orders dominate.

Check the kitchen, worker-hours and demand before accepting the target

Suppose the team has four people, 40 hours each per week, 75% productive utilization and 0.25 total worker-hours per order. The calculator's annualized monthly convention gives 4 × 40 × (52 ÷ 12) × 75% ÷ 0.25 = 2,080 orders of worker-hour capacity. “Total worker-hours” means combined work across staff, not simply the elapsed time a ticket spends in the kitchen.

The 1,800-order base target fits that entered capacity at roughly 86.5%. The 2,094-order food-cost case is already 14 orders over it, and the 4,427-order delivery case is far beyond it. More staff or equipment may change both capacity and cost; rerun the financial requirement after adding those commitments.

Worker-hour capacity is only one constraint. Check peak tickets per hour at the slowest station, preparation capacity, delivery collection congestion, opening days, seats and realistic table turns. Separately test whether demand can produce the required orders. A monthly total can fit while a Friday dinner service still fails.

Keep cash planning separate too: rent deposits, debt principal, equipment purchases and settlement delays can create a cash shortage even when the included operating costs break even. Use the restaurant budgeting guide to build the broader operating plan.

A repeatable owner checklist for your next planning review

  1. Define the unit and period. Choose orders, meals or covers, keep all inputs consistent, and use the same month for commitments and revenue.
  2. Use records before assumptions. Take sales and discounts from the POS, ingredients from recipes and inventory, fees from settlement statements, and committed payroll and rent from actual obligations.
  3. Reconcile the variable-cost boundary. Include packaging, incremental labor and order-related promotions. Check that payment fees, delivery commission and wastage appear once each.
  4. Separate three targets. Record operating break-even, owner-pay funding and additional profit funding so a busy month does not hide an unpaid owner.
  5. Stress the weakest assumptions. Test ingredient increases, discounts and a delivery-heavy mix. Keep the same unit and requirement when comparing scenarios.
  6. Check capacity at service level. Compare the target with worker-hours, the slowest kitchen station and realistic demand, then update costs for any expansion.
  7. Compare actuals with the plan. Review orders, contribution per order and cost changes after the month closes. Preserve the input date and source records so another person can reproduce the calculation.

If you want to assess the tool itself, time the same defined task manually and with the calculator, use identical inputs, check both outputs and record errors as well as minutes. Report device, task, units and assumptions alongside any score. That produces more useful evidence than a general “easy to use” rating.

Frequently asked questions

Is this an independent restaurant calculator review?

No. MyBreakeven published this review using feedback supplied from five Malaysian restaurant owners. Raw participant worksheets, individual timing records and independently audited restaurant profits were not supplied.

Does the reported 87.5% reduction mean restaurant profits increased?

No. It is the relative reduction from a reported 120 minutes to 15 minutes of calculation time: (120 − 15) ÷ 120 × 100. Actual profit improvements were not measured.

Can I use the restaurant break-even calculator with Malaysian ringgit?

The break-even arithmetic works with RM when every monetary input uses RM and the same time period. The worked example here uses RM throughout; it does not demonstrate Malaysian tax, SST, payroll or accounting compliance.

Should I plan in orders or customers?

Use one unit throughout. An order can include several guests, so average revenue and variable costs per order cannot be combined with a guest-count target. Convert orders into covers only with a stated guests-per-order assumption.

How do delivery app fees affect restaurant break-even?

Subtract percentage fees and order-specific packaging or promotional costs from revenue before calculating contribution. The hypothetical RM30 delivery case here leaves RM6.10 contribution and needs 4,427 orders to fund RM27,000; actual contract terms will differ.

Can a profitable-looking sales target exceed restaurant capacity?

Yes. Positive contribution does not guarantee enough worker-hours, kitchen throughput, seats or customer demand. Compare the target with each relevant constraint and recalculate any extra fixed or variable costs required to expand capacity.

Are food-wastage analysis and seats-per-service planning verified premium features?

This review does not establish that. The verified product scope is described separately from participant requests and promotional artwork. Dedicated food-wastage accounting and physical seat-capacity checks were not verified in this review.
