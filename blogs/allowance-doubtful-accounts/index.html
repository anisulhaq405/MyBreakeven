Allowance for Doubtful Accounts: Estimate and Reconcile

Quick answer

An allowance for doubtful accounts reduces gross receivables to a supported carrying amount for expected credit losses. Estimate the required closing balance, then reconcile the existing allowance before calculating the provision; the required balance is not automatically this period’s expense.

Read the source-backed application and limits. Worked business amounts are fictional teaching examples.

All amounts and operating results below are original fictional teaching examples in USD. They are not customer results, industry averages or forecasts. Use your own dated records and applicable accounting policies.

State the reporting framework and current policy choices

This educational example uses US GAAP credit-loss terminology for ordinary trade receivables. It is not a complete ASC 326 model or a substitute for entity-specific accounting review. Aging percentages are fictional assumptions, not prescribed rates. Assess relevant experience, current conditions, customer-specific evidence and the applicable forecasting requirements.

FASB ASU 2025-05 introduced optional simplifications for in-scope current receivables and contract assets. A current-conditions practical expedient and, for qualifying nonpublic entities using it, a subsequent-collections policy election require their own scope and disclosure assessment. The example below assumes neither election. Do not infer that every entity must use the same forecast or subsequent-cash treatment.

The three amounts reconcile to $80,000 gross AR. The required closing allowance is $500 + $800 + $1,500 = $2,800, giving a $77,200 net carrying amount. Review whether similar risk characteristics support the grouping and whether any balance needs separate assessment. Avoid applying both a pooled rate and a second specific allowance to the same exposure without reconciling the overlap.

A bucket percentage must have a defensible basis and an update process in a real model. The arithmetic alone does not establish a compliant loss estimate. Preserve how credits, disputes, write-offs and subsequent evidence affect the population and rates.

Reconcile the existing allowance before recording the provision

Opening credit $1,200 − $700 write-offs + $200 recovery reinstatement leaves $700 credit before the new provision. To reach $2,800, record a $2,100 provision: Dr Credit Loss / Bad Debt Expense, Cr Allowance. Recording the entire $2,800 as new expense would overstate this example’s provision by $700.

If the pre-adjustment allowance instead had a $300 debit balance, reaching $2,800 credit would require a $3,100 credit provision. Keep debit and credit signs visible; subtracting an unsigned balance can produce the wrong answer.

Reconcile gross and net AR as well as the allowance

For the same fictional period, opening gross AR is $60,000. Add $40,000 credit sales, deduct $19,300 ordinary collections and $700 write-offs, reinstate $200 recovered receivable and collect that $200. Closing gross AR is $80,000. Total collections are $19,500, including the recovery; do not deduct that recovery twice.

Opening net AR is $60,000 − $1,200 = $58,800. Add $40,000 credit sales, deduct $19,500 cash collections and $2,100 provision to get $77,200 closing net AR. A write-off against an existing allowance reduces gross AR and allowance together, so it does not by itself create a second net-AR loss in this model.

Keep approval, evidence and estimation separate

Support write-offs and recoveries with approved customer records. Reconcile the detailed customer ledger to the gross AR control, and the allowance schedule to its own control account. A customer promise is not collected cash; a write-off is not a cash payment. Document the estimate owner, review date and changes in assumptions.

Use the receivable workflow for invoice detail and the bad-debt expense guide for the event-by-event provision, write-off and recovery entries. The current-assets guide explains gross versus net presentation. Tax deductibility is a separate determination and is not established by an allowance entry.

Apply the current policy scope before treating an aging table as an estimate

The official FASB ASU 2025-05 summary states that the optional changes apply to specified current receivables and contract assets arising under Topic 606. The effective annual periods begin after December 15, 2025, with early adoption permitted. This case does not elect the options; its arithmetic is not evidence that another entity’s population or policy qualifies.

Before using a real aging matrix, reconcile the population to the gross receivable control, separate credit balances and disputed items for review, and record how due dates are assigned. Keep the loss-rate support, risk grouping, current information and any forecasting assessment together with the calculation. A rate copied from an online article is an unsupported input even when the multiplication is exact. A customer’s overdue status and an approved write-off are also different facts.

The $2,800 target is a closing allowance balance. The current $2,100 provision follows from the existing $700 credit after the specified write-off and recovery events. If the pre-adjustment allowance were already higher than the supported target, investigate the evidence and policy rather than forcing another positive expense simply because a table has a positive estimated loss. Keep a signed rollforward: opening allowance, provision or release, write-offs, recoveries and other explained movements. Reconcile the net receivable as well as the gross population so an adjustment is not counted twice.

Frequently asked questions

Is the required allowance the same as this period’s expense?

Not necessarily. Reconcile the existing debit or credit balance and intervening movements first.

Are the 1%, 4% and 15% rates recommended?

No. They are fictional inputs to demonstrate arithmetic, not a compliant estimate for another business.

Does writing off an already provided balance create another expense?

Under this allowance example, it reduces gross AR and allowance together rather than recognizing the expense twice.

Does the guide apply every current US GAAP simplification?

No. The case assumes neither ASU 2025-05 election; eligibility, consistency and disclosures require separate review.

Do the optional FASB changes cover every financial asset?

No. ASU 2025-05 describes a specified scope for current receivables and contract assets arising from Topic 606 transactions.

Must every adjustment to an allowance increase expense?

No. Compare the supported closing requirement with the signed existing balance and movements; the evidence can require a provision or a release under the applicable policy.
