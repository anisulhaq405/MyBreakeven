Quick answer

The accounting cycle connects transaction evidence to journal entries, ledger accounts, adjustments, statements and closing. Keep the unadjusted, adjusted and post-closing balances separate so each stage has a traceable purpose.

Read the source-backed application and limits. Worked business amounts are fictional teaching examples.

All amounts and operating results below are original fictional teaching examples in USD. They are not customer results, industry averages or forecasts. Use your own dated records and applicable accounting policies.

Record six supported events before adjusting

This fictional sole-proprietor service business begins with zero balances. The owner invests $10,000 cash. It buys $900 supplies on account, earns and invoices $4,000 of accepted services, collects $2,500 of that receivable, pays $600 rent for the month, and receives $1,200 for future services. No tax, depreciation, inventory for resale or owner drawings are included.

Journal entries preserve the two-sided effect of each event. For example, supplies purchased on account debit Supplies and credit Accounts Payable by $900. Collecting $2,500 debits Cash and credits Accounts Receivable; it does not record another sale. Post each journal reference into the appropriate ledger accounts.

Keep the adjustment evidence distinct

At month end, a supported count shows $600 supplies remain, so $300 has been used. Employees have earned $700 unpaid wages. Of the $1,200 advance, $400 is now earned under the case’s service-recognition assumptions. Record three adjustments: Dr Supplies Expense / Cr Supplies $300; Dr Wages Expense / Cr Wages Payable $700; Dr Customer Advance Liability / Cr Service Revenue $400.

These entries change the period’s result and balances without creating a cash transaction at the adjustment date. Preserve the count, payroll cutoff and delivery evidence. Adjusting to a desired profit number is not a valid substitute for that support.

The unadjusted trial balance totals $16,100 on each side. After adjustments, both sides total $16,800. Equal totals show posting equality, not proof that the right accounts and periods were used. An omitted event or a wrong but balanced entry can escape this test.

Prepare statements from the adjusted balances

Recognized revenue is $4,400. Expenses are $600 rent + $300 supplies + $700 wages = $1,600, leaving $2,800 profit. Closing equity before any drawings is $10,000 investment + $2,800 profit = $12,800. Assets are $13,100 cash + $1,500 receivables + $600 supplies = $15,200. Liabilities are $900 AP + $700 wages + $800 unearned advance = $2,400. The balance sheet reconciles: $15,200 = $2,400 + $12,800.

Cash increased $13,100 from zero, which differs from profit because owner funding, collections, credit purchases and the customer advance have different effects. The cash-flow statement and reporting framework require their own classification; do not copy the profit subtotal into a cash balance.

Close temporary accounts without deleting history

For this illustrative sole-proprietor closing convention, debit Revenue $4,400 and credit Income Summary. Debit Income Summary $1,600 and credit the three expense accounts. Transfer the remaining $2,800 credit from Income Summary to Owner Capital. Revenue, expenses and Income Summary then have zero balances; capital is $12,800. There are no drawings to close in this case.

The post-closing trial balance contains $15,200 debit assets and $15,200 credit liabilities/equity. Real systems may automate closing or use different equity accounts; do not post these manual entries on top of an automated close. Preserve the transaction history and the reports used for approval.

Use each report for its own question

The journal explains what was recorded; the ledger groups postings by account. The trial balance checks aggregate equality; the statements report the resulting position and performance. The bookkeeping routine gathers evidence and reconciles detail. Accrual accounting explains why dates can differ.

Keep the prior period’s closing balances as the next period’s opening balances and record correction references. Use statement analysis after the close to interpret the result, rather than treating interpretation as another posting step.

Use a stage-by-stage completion test before carrying balances forward

The OpenStax closing-entry explanation separates temporary revenue and expense accounts from permanent balance-sheet accounts. The teaching case uses owner capital because it is a sole-proprietor illustration. A corporation may use retained earnings and different distribution accounts. Follow the entity and system actually used instead of copying equity-account names without checking them.

Treat each stage as a separate control. The unadjusted trial balance must trace to posted journals. Each adjustment needs its own evidence and reference. Statements must reconcile to the adjusted balances. The closing journals must reset only the intended temporary accounts. Finally, permanent balances must agree with the next period’s opening records. A list of stage names without these links does not demonstrate a completed close.

In this case, $4,400 revenue less $1,600 expenses produces $2,800 profit before closing. Transferring that amount to capital changes where the result is recorded within equity; it does not create another $2,800 of business performance. The $15,200 post-closing debit total is smaller than the $16,800 adjusted debit total because the expense balances have been closed, not because $1,600 cash disappeared at closing. Keep both reports so that difference can be explained. Reversing selected accruals, when the system uses that procedure, is also separate from closing all temporary accounts.

Frequently asked questions

Why prepare more than one trial balance?

They show distinct stages: before adjustments, after adjustments and after temporary accounts are closed.

Does a balanced trial balance prove correctness?

No. Omissions, wrong classification and incorrect timing can still leave equal totals.

Does closing delete old transactions?

No. It resets temporary balances under the selected closing convention while preserving history.

Should I manually close software that already closes automatically?

Do not duplicate automated closing. Follow the actual system and reporting policy.

Why do adjusted and post-closing trial-balance totals differ?

Temporary accounts have been closed. In this case the $1,600 expense debit balances disappear from the post-closing trial balance while the result is transferred to equity.

Does transferring profit to capital earn the profit again?

No. It transfers an already measured result within the accounting records; it is not a new revenue or cash event.
