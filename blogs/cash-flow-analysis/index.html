Cash Flow Analysis: Operating Quality, Funding and Liquidity

Cash-flow analysis explains where historical cash came from, what used it and whether the pattern appears repeatable. Reconcile opening and closing cash, examine operating, investing and financing flows separately, then investigate profit conversion and funding dependence. Use a separate dated forecast for future payments; a positive past cash balance does not establish future liquidity.

All numerical examples are original fictional USD teaching cases, not market benchmarks, customer results or personalized professional advice. Replace assumptions with dated records and identify the accounting framework being used.

Begin with a decision and a reconciled historical record

Write the business question before choosing a ratio: did trading generate cash, was an equipment purchase funded sustainably, or is a large balance mainly the result of borrowing? Specify the period, entity, currency and cash definition. Keep restricted amounts or money unavailable for ordinary obligations clearly identified rather than assuming every reported dollar is spendable.

Obtain the cash-flow statement, comparative balance sheets, income statement and supporting schedules. The cash-flow statement guide owns preparation and classification. This article starts with those records and asks what they mean. If the cash change does not reconcile, resolve that difference before describing the trend as improvement or deterioration.

Confirm whether the record includes unusual collections, delayed supplier payments, asset disposals, new debt, owner contributions or currency effects. The total change in cash combines several causes. A single positive number can conceal weak trading, while a cash decrease can reflect a deliberate funded investment rather than an operating failure.

Read a checked operating, investing and financing bridge

In an original standalone annual USD case, net income is $18,000 and depreciation is $6,000. Customer receivables increase $11,000, inventory increases $4,000 and operating supplier payables increase $3,000. Assume these are the only indirect adjustments. Operating cash is $18,000 + $6,000 − $11,000 − $4,000 + $3,000 = $12,000.

The business pays $22,000 for equipment, receives $15,000 from a new loan, repays $4,000 of loan principal and pays $3,000 of owner distributions. Under the stated teaching classification, investing cash is −$22,000 and financing cash is $15,000 − $4,000 − $3,000 = $8,000. The total cash change is therefore −$2,000.

Investigate profit conversion instead of blaming the total

Operating cash is $6,000 below the case’s $18,000 net income. Depreciation increases the cash bridge by $6,000 because it is noncash in this period, but the combined working-capital effect is −$12,000: receivables and inventory use $15,000 while unpaid suppliers provide $3,000. The $6,000 net difference is therefore explained by specific balances.

Ask why customer receivables increased. Higher credit sales, slower collections, disputed invoices and cutoff errors can produce different responses. Review invoice dates, payment terms, aging, credits and subsequent receipts. An assumption that all receivables are collectible is not validated by a positive operating-cash total or a successful bank reconciliation.

Investigate inventory purchases and supplier timing on the same basis. Stock prepared for a documented seasonal order differs from obsolete inventory; normal payment terms differ from overdue balances that threaten supply. Net income explains the period profit boundary, while working-capital analysis connects the relevant current assets and liabilities.

Separate investment funding from recurring cash generation

The equipment payment is $22,000, exceeding the period’s $12,000 operating cash by $10,000. Define a simple, explicitly limited cash-after-equipment measure as operating cash less these equipment payments: $12,000 − $22,000 = −$10,000. This is not a universal free-cash-flow definition and does not include every possible financing or investment need.

The $8,000 net financing inflow partly covers that $10,000 gap, leaving the $2,000 decrease in bank cash. Removing the new loan receipt alone, with every other case item unchanged, would leave closing cash at −$1,000. That counterfactual identifies dependence on the receipt; it does not prove that a different operating or investment plan was impossible.

Use the CAPEX guide to distinguish equipment payments from depreciation and the expansion guide to test the commercial investment case. Investment can be appropriate even when it reduces current cash, but its timing, funding conditions and future operating effects need explicit support rather than a label such as “growth spending.”

Do not confuse a year-end balance with payment-date liquidity

The $14,000 closing balance does not describe the lowest balance during the year or guarantee that a large payment just before collections could be made. Compare dated receipts and obligations in the cash-flow forecast template. The forecast owns forward timing; this historical analysis supplies reconciled facts and questions for its assumptions.

Run a separate timing sensitivity only after labeling it as a scenario. If $5,000 of collections shift to the following period while all other historical-case flows are held constant, the scenario closing balance would be $9,000. That arithmetic isolates one delay; it is not a prediction or evidence that the business had access to replacement credit.

Check restrictions, minimum operating reserves, tax obligations and financing conditions using actual documents. A cash account may contain a customer deposit linked to future delivery, and a credit facility may have conditions before money can be drawn. Do not add an assumed facility limit to cash or treat an unapproved refinancing as a completed financing inflow.

Convert the analysis into an evidence-led action record

Compare several compatible periods and explain changes in perimeter, classification or accounting estimates. A single annual comparison may hide seasonal movements. Keep the original records, then show any analytical regrouping separately. Standards and business circumstances can affect interest, tax or other classifications; define the approach used rather than treating every presentation as identical.

Use the financial-reporting workflow to resolve incomplete schedules and cloud-accounting controls to make source transactions and corrections traceable. In a new venture, startup-cost classification may explain why large opening payments differ from recognized expenses. An LLC owner contribution is funding, not proof of operating demand.

Assign each finding an evidence owner, deadline and next decision. Examples include collecting disputed invoices, validating inventory demand, confirming an equipment payment date or reviewing supplier obligations. Preserve the boundary between facts and proposed scenarios. The best next action addresses the identified cause; a generic instruction to increase sales does not resolve an invoice dispute or a funding condition.

Keep a reviewable decision record

Open the editable, printable review record. Record the period, entity, evidence, calculation, unresolved issue and next action. This manual record does not automatically calculate, save entries after reload or submit information. Use non-sensitive assumptions and print or save a copy before leaving the page.

Frequently asked questions

What is cash-flow analysis?

It interprets historical sources and uses of cash, profit conversion, funding dependence and liquidity evidence after the underlying records have been reconciled.

Is it the same as a cash-flow statement?

No. The statement presents the historical movements. Analysis investigates their causes, repeatability and implications for a decision.

Can a profitable business lose cash?

Yes. Receivables, inventory, equipment, debt repayment and distributions can use cash even when the income statement reports a profit.

Is borrowing positive operating cash flow?

No. In the stated teaching case new borrowing is financing cash. It can increase bank cash without demonstrating operating cash generation.

Is operating cash less CAPEX always free cash flow?

No single label settles the definition. This guide states a limited cash-after-equipment measure and explicitly identifies what it includes and excludes.

Does a positive closing balance prove future liquidity?

No. Future payment dates, restrictions, reserves and financing conditions require a separate dated forecast and supporting documents.
