Cost per Hire vs Employee Cost: Two Different Budgets

Published November 4, 2026Updated November 4, 2026

Calculate recruiting cost per completed hire, then build a first-year employment budget. Include internal time consistently without double-counting onboarding.

Cost per hire divides the recruiting costs included in a defined period by completed hires in that period. Employee cost measures the ongoing cost of employing someone. The two budgets answer different questions and should not be added without checking shared items.

Every amount in the worked case is an original fictional USD planning assumption. It is not an industry benchmark, a customer result or a prediction.

Define the recruiting numerator and the hires denominator

Cost per hire = (included external recruiting costs + included internal recruiting costs) ÷ completed hires. External costs may include job ads, agency fees and screening. Internal costs may include recruiting staff and documented interviewer time. Use one written cost policy so months and roles can be compared.

Count actual hires using a consistent event, such as accepted offers or starts, and disclose which event you use. An open vacancy is not a hire. When a period has costs but zero hires, the ratio is undefined; report the spend and zero denominator rather than returning $0 cost per hire. Multi-month campaigns need a reconciliation so costs and outcomes are not silently moved between periods.

The hourly values are planning assumptions, not current pay benchmarks. If interviewer salary is already in the internal recruiting pool, do not add the same hours again. A cash-only view might show $3,000 external spend, but it omits $2,400 of valued staff effort; label the two views rather than comparing them as identical metrics.

Build the employment budget separately

For one illustrative role, assume $48,000 salary, $4,800 employer charges, $3,600 benefits, $1,200 equipment/software and $1,000 onboarding paid time outside the recruiting definition. The first-year employment budget before recruitment is $58,600. Adding the separately assigned $2,700 recruitment cost gives $61,300.

These are chosen amounts, not a payroll tax or benefit rate. A hiring bonus, relocation payment, training time or recruiter salary must appear only once under the adopted boundary. Recurring-year cost can differ from first-year cost because recruiting and equipment are not necessarily repeated.

Distinguish hiring efficiency from hiring quality

A lower cost per hire does not prove a better hire. Compare like roles and channels, then review acceptance, start dates, retention and observed job performance. A cheap process with repeated replacement hires can cost more overall. Small samples also move sharply: one additional start changes a two-hire denominator by 50%.

Time to fill, recruiting conversion yield and cost per productive hour answer other questions. A team may hire quickly but lack work, onboarding capacity or cash to support the position. Use the employee cost calculator for recurring and productive-hour economics, and the dated cash forecast for the payroll funding period.

Keep a recruiting ledger that can be reproduced

  1. Choose the reporting period and the counted hire event.
  2. List invoices and internal hours with an owner and source reference.
  3. Document shared-cost allocation and any excluded costs.
  4. Reconcile hires, costs, open campaigns and timing differences.
  5. Compare channels only after matching cost and role definitions.

Keep applicant records secure and use job-related review criteria. The financial worksheet does not determine employment-law obligations, eligibility or candidate suitability.

Frequently asked questions

Does cost per hire include annual salary?

The employee’s ongoing annual salary belongs in the employment budget, not automatically in recruiting cost. Recruiting staff effort can be part of internal recruiting costs.

What if no one was hired?

Report recruiting spend and zero completed hires. Division by zero does not produce a meaningful cost-per-hire result.

How should internal interview time be valued?

Use a documented, consistently applied hourly cost or internal rate. Keep it separate from cash-only recruiting spend and avoid adding the same staff cost twice.

Is lower cost per hire always better?

No. Role mix, timing, sample size, retention and hiring outcomes affect the comparison. A low ratio can conceal repeated replacement hiring.
