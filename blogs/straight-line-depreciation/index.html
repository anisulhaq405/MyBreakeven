Straight-Line Depreciation: Formula, Partial Periods and Schedule

Straight-line depreciation allocates the depreciable amount evenly across the selected time-based useful life. Annual allocation equals cost minus residual value, divided by life in years; a partial-period schedule must use an explicit start date and time convention.

All amounts and operating results below are original fictional teaching examples in USD. They are not customer results, industry averages or forecasts. Use your own dated records and applicable accounting policies.

Define cost, life, residual and in-use date

For this independent service-asset case, capitalized cost is $14,000, residual value $2,000 and useful life five years. The asset is available for intended use on April 1, 2027. The example adopts a whole-month convention beginning that date and assumes no impairment, disposal, method switch or estimate change. These are teaching assumptions, not mandatory asset lives or a universal partial-month rule.

The purchase invoice can precede readiness for use. Keep evidence of the in-use date and explain any capitalization policy. Tax conventions can differ from this financial-reporting month count; do not automatically apply a tax half-year or mid-quarter convention to this schedule.

Compute the annual and monthly amounts once

Depreciable amount = $14,000 − $2,000 = $12,000. Annual straight-line depreciation = $12,000 / 5 = $2,400. Monthly amount = $2,400 / 12 = $200. Equivalently, $12,000 / 60 months = $200 per month. The full-year rate is 20% of the depreciable amount, not 20% of cost without considering residual value.

April through December includes nine months under the stated convention, so 2027 allocation is $200 × 9 = $1,800. Nine months is not a universal rule for every April purchase; it follows this exact in-use date and policy.

The six calendar rows cover only five years of useful life: 9 + 12 + 12 + 12 + 12 + 3 = 60 months. Four full intervening calendar years allocate $9,600. The first $1,800 and final $600 complete the $12,000 depreciable amount. Closing carrying amount stops at the $2,000 residual; do not charge another full $2,400 in 2032.

Post the allocation without inventing another cash payment

If the first nine months are all period expense in this service example, debit Depreciation Expense and credit Accumulated Depreciation by $1,800 for 2027. The original asset cost remains $14,000, accumulated depreciation becomes $1,800 and carrying amount is $12,200. No cash leaves the bank merely because that entry is posted.

Production assets may contribute depreciation to inventory costs instead. The example does not prescribe that classification for every business. Keep acquisition cash, capitalization, current allocation and eventual expense recognition traceable rather than automatically treating them as one payment.

Handle changes and rounding with a documented policy

Reassess estimates when supported facts change. A change in remaining life or residual value may change future allocation; do not silently rewrite old months or automatically treat an estimate revision as an error. An actual disposal or impairment also requires its own analysis. Use the applicable framework and approval process.

For a monthly amount that is not exact to cents, retain calculation precision and reconcile the final allocation to the intended residual. Do not round every input repeatedly and then allow the schedule to drift below its floor. This worksheet is manual and does not enforce that check automatically.

Frequently asked questions

Why does a five-year asset appear in six calendar years?

The first and last calendar years are partial. The schedule still contains exactly sixty months.

Does straight line use cost without residual value?

The depreciable amount is cost less the supported residual estimate.

Is purchase date always the depreciation start date?

Not necessarily. Check readiness for intended use and the applicable reporting policy.

Does this calculate a tax deduction?

No. It is a financial-reporting illustration with a stated time convention.
