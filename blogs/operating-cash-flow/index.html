Operating cash flow (OCF) is the cash generated or used by operating activities during a stated period. It is also called cash flow from operations or CFO. It separates day-to-day business cash generation from investing and financing movements. Revenue, net income and EBITDA each measure something different; none can simply be relabeled as OCF.

Cedar Supply, our fictional reseller, reports $100,000 March sales and $9,000 net income but only $2,000 operating cash flow. Below, actual receipts minus payments and a net-income reconciliation reach the same amount. All USD inputs are invented for instruction. The example uses a simplified US-style nonfinancial-business policy with interest and income tax paid in operations, no interest payable movement, and no foreign-exchange or noncash balance changes.

Operating cash flow formula: choose the presentation

Direct OCF = operating cash receipts − operating cash payments. Use actual collections rather than booked sales and actual payments rather than recognized expenses. Keep capital purchases and debt principal outside that operating subtotal under the example policy.

Indirect OCF = net income + included noncash adjustments − increases in operating assets + increases in operating liabilities + other signed reconciliation adjustments. Decreases reverse the signs. “Other adjustments” may remove investing or financing gains/expenses according to the reporting framework. The formula is a reconciliation structure, not permission to include cash, borrowings or every current account in working capital.

For the full operating/investing/financing layout, use the cash flow statement guide. This page focuses on calculating and diagnosing its operating section.

Do not use total net working capital from a balance sheet mechanically. It may include cash, debt, tax or non-operating items with different classification requirements. A supplier-finance arrangement also needs its actual terms and the applicable reporting rules reviewed. The working-capital calculation guide addresses the balance-sheet liquidity measure; the operating bridge is a narrower cash-reconciliation task.

Is positive or negative operating cash flow good?

Positive OCF means this period’s included operating receipts exceed its operating payments. It does not prove bills were paid on time or that the result will repeat. In Cedar Supply, the $8,000 combined increase in supplier and payroll obligations supports the cash result. A separate scenario with those increases removed and everything else unchanged would produce −$6,000 OCF. That scenario isolates payment timing; it does not claim the suppliers have agreed to any terms.

Negative OCF can accompany a planned inventory build or receivables growth, but also recurring losses or poor collections. Identify the cause, the dated funding need and the evidence of eventual recovery. Repeated borrowing to cover negative contribution does not repair the underlying economics. Use the cash-flow diagnosis and recovery example to separate a timing gap from a business-model problem. There is no universal healthy OCF threshold for every company or month.

Frequently asked questions

What is the operating cash flow formula?

The direct approach subtracts operating cash payments from operating receipts. A net-income indirect bridge adds included noncash adjustments, subtracts operating asset increases, adds operating liability increases and includes other required signed adjustments under the stated policy.

Why is an increase in receivables subtracted?

In the clean example, recognized sales exceed collections by the receivables increase. Subtracting that increase converts the accrual sales effect to cash. Noncash or acquisition changes need separate reconciliation.

Is depreciation a source of operating cash?

No. Adding back depreciation reverses an expense that reduced net income without a current cash payment. It does not create a new receipt.

Can operating cash flow be negative when net income is positive?

Yes. Inventory, receivables, prepayments and payments of older obligations can absorb more cash than current profits provide. Identify timing, balance movements and recurring operating economics.

Does the checker classify accounts or verify compliance?

No. It checks entered signed adjustments and compares them with a separately entered direct operating total. You must verify the dates, reporting policy and completeness of the source records.
