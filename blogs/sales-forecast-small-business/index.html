Quick answer

A practical sales forecast sums expected units multiplied by net selling price for each offer and period. Build the unit assumptions from customer evidence, check delivery capacity, and keep revenue recognition and collection dates separate.

Read the source-backed application and limits. Worked business amounts are fictional teaching examples.

Every amount in the worked case is an original fictional USD planning assumption. It is not an industry benchmark, a customer result or a prediction.

Choose a method that matches the evidence you have

For repeat services, begin with opening customers, expected completed work and known renewals. For a product line, use expected sold units and net unit prices. For a qualified pipeline, sum opportunity value multiplied by a documented close probability for the relevant period. Historical growth can supply a baseline, but unexplained percentage growth is not customer evidence.

Forecast sales = Σ(expected units × net unit price). A weighted pipeline is an expectation across uncertain outcomes, not a promise that a fractional contract will close. Do not add the same sale again when it appears in both a customer schedule and the pipeline.

The upside is a demand case, not an executable delivery forecast. It needs 32 more productive hours, a revised offer mix or rescheduling. If overtime or contractors add cost, update contribution and cash along with sales. If cancellations are already incorporated in completed-job assumptions, do not subtract them a second time.

Separate signed work, delivered revenue and cash received

A signed $3,000 agreement in November might be delivered in December and collected in January. Keep separate columns for signed bookings, the revenue period under the applicable accounting treatment, and the expected receipt date. The illustrative tables use completed service work as the sales basis; they are not tax-method guidance.

Once sales are built, send dated receipts into the cash-flow forecast. Use the rolling forecast guide to update the horizon as actual months close. These are linked tasks, not interchangeable templates.

Measure forecast error and improve the assumptions

Freeze a dated forecast version before the month begins. Compare actual units, net price, mix, cancellations and timing against that version. If actual revenue is $10,200 against $10,800, the dollar error is −$600 and the shortfall versus forecast is 5.56%. This stated denominator is forecast revenue; a metric using actual revenue would produce a different percentage.

Review persistent bias rather than rewriting the old forecast to match actuals. For a new business, replace weak assumptions with paid pilots, signed scope, customer interviews and observed conversion rates as evidence accumulates. Retain the source date and owner of each assumption in the business plan evidence register.

Calibrate pipeline probability and reconcile it to delivery

Salesforce’s forecasting guidance recommends maintaining opportunity detail and using historical evidence when adjusting close probabilities. A stage label alone does not prove a reliable chance of winning. Record when each estimate was made, the expected close date and any unresolved condition.

Consider an additional fictional pipeline: one $10,000 opportunity at an assumed 30% close probability and another $20,000 opportunity at 60%. Their weighted total is $15,000. Actual bookings from these two all-or-nothing opportunities could be $0, $10,000, $20,000 or $30,000. The $15,000 expectation is neither a partial signed contract nor a receipt guaranteed for next month. Correlated customer decisions can also make a downside scenario more severe than the weighted total suggests.

Assign probable delivery dates only after checking the work. The existing two-offer base already requires all 128 productive worker-hours. Adding a newly won contract to that month without moving another job or obtaining supported additional capacity would overstate executable revenue. Preserve a demand view and a feasible delivery view, then send the feasible invoices and payment terms into cash planning.

After closing the period, compare the dated opportunity list with wins, losses, slippages and delivery evidence. Investigate systematically optimistic close dates or stage probabilities. Keep the original forecast unchanged so later edits do not erase the forecast error being measured.

Frequently asked questions

Can a sales target be used as a forecast?

A target states an ambition. A forecast estimates an outcome from stated evidence and assumptions. Keep both visible rather than treating the target as proof.

Does the forecast show cash available?

Not by itself. Put delivery, invoice and collection dates into a separate dated cash schedule.

How should I handle an upside above capacity?

Label it a demand scenario and calculate the additional worker-hours or other constrained resources before treating it as deliverable revenue.

What error denominator does the example use?

It divides the revenue shortfall by forecast revenue. State the denominator because using actual revenue produces another percentage.

Does $15,000 weighted pipeline mean $15,000 will be delivered next month?

No. It is an expectation from stated opportunity values and assumed probabilities. Wins, timing, recognition and delivery capacity need separate evidence.

Can I add won pipeline to the base without another capacity check?

Only if the original base did not already include those jobs and sufficient delivery resources remain. This example’s base uses all 128 productive worker-hours.
