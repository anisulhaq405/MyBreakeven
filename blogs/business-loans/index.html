A business loan supplies cash that must be repaid under a contract. Compare what reaches the business, every required payment and the cash source for repayment. A lower monthly payment can come with a longer commitment and higher total cost. Start with the use of funds before comparing lenders or headline rates.

All numerical examples are original fictional USD teaching cases. They are not market rates, customer results, eligibility determinations or forecasts. Replace assumptions with dated records.

Match the borrowing structure to the business use

A term loan generally provides a defined advance repaid over an agreed schedule. A line of credit permits draws within an approved facility, subject to its terms and availability. Equipment financing links funding to an asset purchase; an asset-backed facility may depend on eligible collateral. The financing label alone does not establish affordability, approval, legal classification or whether a personal guarantee is required.

Compare offers on the same amount and dates

Record gross principal, fees withheld, fees paid separately, net usable proceeds, the entire payment schedule, any final balloon, interest basis and security requirements. Keep an offer expiry date and link each amount to the written document. A quote that funds $20,000 and one that leaves $19,600 after a fee do not provide the same usable cash.

Do not compare a factor rate, a flat fee and an annual interest rate as if they were the same measure. Ask for the total repayment and a supported annualized disclosure when available; the calculation method and included charges matter. Faster approval or a lower advertised payment does not answer the question of total cost or repayment capacity.

A checked $20,000 term-loan illustration

Assume a fictional $20,000 principal, a fixed nominal annual rate of 12%, twelve equal end-of-month payments, monthly rate 1%, and no balloon. A separate $400 origination fee is withheld at funding. This is a teaching assumption, not a lender offer or quoted APR. Usable funding is $19,600.

For principal P, monthly rate r and n payments, payment = P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)−n]. At zero interest, use P ÷ n. Here the unrounded payment is $1,776.9757735668…, displayed as $1,776.98. Multiplying the unrounded amount by twelve gives $21,323.71 total repayment after presentation rounding. Interest is $1,323.71; adding the $400 fee gives $1,723.71 modeled borrowing cost relative to usable proceeds. Actual cent-rounded servicing schedules can adjust the final payment.

The $400 withheld fee is included once in the proceeds comparison. Do not subtract it again as a later cash payment unless the agreement actually requires another charge. Likewise, repayment principal is not automatically an operating expense in a profit-margin calculation.

Why a longer term can lower payments but increase cost

With the same hypothetical principal and 12% nominal rate, extending the schedule to 24 end-of-month payments produces an unrounded $941.4694444653… payment. The displayed monthly burden falls to $941.47, but unrounded total payments rise to $22,595.27. With the same $400 withheld fee, modeled total borrowing cost becomes $2,995.27, versus $1,723.71 for twelve months. The lower payment does not make the longer option cheaper.

This comparison holds the rate, principal and fee constant to isolate term. Real offers can change all three. A variable-rate agreement also needs a rate-stress scenario and the exact reset terms. Do not extend the life of borrowing merely to make a spreadsheet’s first month look comfortable without reviewing the full commitment.

Test repayment against cash available on the payment date

Suppose the business expects $2,300 monthly cash available after operating payments and its planned owner cash need, before this new debt. A $1,776.98 payment leaves about $523.02. If collections are $700 lower while other payments stay the same, only $1,600 is available and the payment creates a roughly $176.98 shortfall. This is a deliberately defined cash test, not a lender’s debt-service ratio or approval rule.

Use a dated cash forecast rather than treating invoices or accounting profit as cash in the bank. Record existing debt, tax dates, asset purchases and minimum reserves separately. Funding proceeds increase cash and debt; they do not prove that an unprofitable offer has become profitable. Cash versus profit break-even explains the boundary.

A proposed collateral value also needs a defined basis. Inventory valuation methods establish accounting costs, but the provider’s eligible borrowing base can differ from inventory carrying value. A grant eligibility review is a separate route and an unawarded application is not a substitute for confirmed repayment cash.

Prepare a decision record before accepting terms

  1. Write the funded purpose, amount and date required, with the expected repayment source.
  2. Reconcile financial records, existing debt, cash forecast and ownership information requested by the provider.
  3. Compare net proceeds and every payment on matching dates, not just the rate label.
  4. Review guarantees, security, default provisions, collection rights, minimum fees and early-exit terms with appropriate support.
  5. Test delayed receipts, lower contribution and any rate reset before making a commitment.

SBA-backed lending is subject to program and lender requirements. The official 7(a) program page describes permitted uses and eligibility context; a government guarantee to a lender is not a grant to the business or an approval promise. This guide does not rank lenders or assume a universal credit-score cutoff.

Frequently asked questions

Is the lowest monthly payment the cheapest business loan?

Not necessarily. A longer schedule can reduce each payment while increasing total interest and the time the business is committed.

Are loan proceeds business revenue?

Borrowed principal generally creates cash and a repayment obligation rather than sales revenue. Keep financing and operating results separate.

Is a flat factor rate the same as APR?

No. A flat multiplier or fee does not describe the timing-sensitive annual cost by itself. Compare the complete schedule and the provider’s supported disclosures.

What if an origination fee is withheld?

Gross principal and usable cash differ. In the example, $20,000 less a $400 withheld fee leaves $19,600, with the fee counted once.

Does SBA backing mean automatic approval?

No. Program and lender criteria still apply. Review the current official program and the written lender requirements.

Can this example determine the loan I should take?

No. It illustrates a comparison method. Your actual terms, security, cash timing and repayment evidence require their own review.
