Quick answer

Start an online business by defining one offer and customer, validating demand, checking the requirements for that activity, and testing a complete payment-to-delivery workflow before scaling. Measure net receipts and delivery capacity alongside sales.

Read the source-backed application and limits. Worked business amounts are fictional teaching examples.

All amounts and operating results below are original fictional teaching examples in USD. They are not customer results, industry averages or forecasts. Use your own dated records and applicable accounting policies.

Write a testable offer and acceptance boundary

Specify the deliverable, exclusions, net price, turnaround, revision limits and how acceptance is recorded. For the fictional pilot here, a $300 service package takes six delivery hours and $40 job-specific cash costs. Record when the buyer can cancel and how a refund or complaint will be handled. Do not promise outcomes that you cannot substantiate.

Use the online model comparison if the model is undecided. This guide starts after that choice. It does not require a custom store: the first workflow can be simple if it supports accurate terms, appropriate records and a reliable way to fulfill the offer.

Five divided by twenty is a 25% conversion for this fictional teaching pilot, not a forecast or a platform benchmark. The $1,050 is not take-home pay. It excludes owner selling time, general administration, fixed subscriptions, tax and setup work. If delivery takes eight hours instead of six, 40 hours produce $26.25 pilot surplus per delivery hour with other inputs unchanged.

At a selected 40-hour delivery ceiling, six-hour jobs permit at most six whole packages, before checking demand. Seven need 42 hours. Record a stop rule for spend, late work, refunds and capacity. A profitable five-order sample does not justify multiplying the result by all website visitors.

Check the activity and selling locations

SBA launch guidance separates registration, IDs, permits, banking and insurance. The IRS startup checklist is explicitly not exhaustive and points to state requirements. Record which official state and local offices apply to your actual business, where customers are served and what evidence remains outstanding. This article does not decide entity choice, tax nexus or professional licensing for you.

For a business operating at home, also check workspace, property and capacity constraints. For US merchandise orders, the FTC explains requirements concerning advertised shipping times, delay consent and refunds. Read the applicable rule before making a shipping promise. Do not treat that merchandise-specific reference as a universal rule for every digital or professional service.

Use authorized test transactions or a processor’s documented test environment; do not fabricate a customer review. Check the workflow on a phone and keyboard. For downloads, open the delivered file on the intended application. For services, test booking and approval. For merchandise, verify packaging, tracking and return handling.

Reconcile the first orders before increasing spend

If the processor settles less than gross customer charges, identify fees, refunds, taxes and timing rather than editing invoice amounts to match the bank. Keep gross activity and settlement deductions traceable. Use the receivable process for unpaid customer invoices and the payable process for supplier obligations.

Prepare a dated cash plan including setup payments, settlement delays and owner withdrawals separately from the defined operating result. For the detailed physical-store funding boundary use the ecommerce startup-cost guide. Continue with measurable business goals only after the delivery system and records are working.

Set separate launch tests for merchandise and service promises

For US merchandise covered by the FTC’s order rule guidance, a seller needs a reasonable basis for its stated shipping time; when no shipping time is stated, the usual rule is shipment within thirty days after a properly completed order. If timely shipment becomes impossible, follow the applicable delay-consent or cancellation/refund procedure. This is not a universal thirty-day delivery rule for every online transaction, and the FTC guidance identifies exemptions and special cases. Services are outside this merchandise rule.

For the original service pilot, make the delivery promise testable: what file or work product is due, how the buyer confirms receipt, which revisions are included and who handles a failed handoff. A payment receipt establishes a payment event; it does not by itself prove completed service performance. Keep the five fictional orders labeled as teaching data when presenting the 25% inquiry conversion. Replace them with genuine dated business records before describing a real customer result. The $1,050 includes a one-off $250 test budget, so it is a defined pilot surplus rather than standard sales-minus-variable-cost contribution margin.

The launch decision should have separate evidence for offer accuracy, payment, fulfillment and customer communications. The FTC review-rule Q&A explains why businesses must avoid fake or false testimonials and incentives conditioned on a particular review sentiment. Ask genuine buyers for their actual experience; a test order, generated persona or fictional worked case must not become a customer endorsement. Record unresolved launch defects with an owner and retest the repaired step before increasing demand.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a custom website to test an offer?

Not necessarily. Use a channel that can present accurate terms, accept appropriate payments and support reliable delivery and records.

Can I forecast a 25% conversion rate from this case?

No. The fraction describes only the fictional twenty-inquiry pilot.

Is processor settlement the same as revenue?

No. Fees, refunds, tax amounts and timing can make the bank deposit differ from recorded sales.

When should I increase advertising?

After checking delivery capacity, net economics, refunds, settlement timing and the quality of repeat demand.

Does the FTC’s usual thirty-day shipping rule cover my consulting service?

The cited rule concerns covered merchandise orders, not services. Service promises still need their own applicable contractual and legal assessment.

Can the fictional five-order pilot be described as a client result?

No. It is a teaching example. Actual result claims need genuine underlying records and an accurate description of what was measured.
