Quick answer

The accounting equation is assets = liabilities + equity. Assets show recognized resources, liabilities show obligations, and equity is the residual interest. Every recorded transaction must preserve the equality even when cash, profit and debt move differently.

Read the source-backed application and limits. Worked business amounts are fictional teaching examples.

All amounts and operating results below are original fictional teaching examples in USD. They are not customer results, industry averages or forecasts. Use your own dated records and applicable accounting policies.

Read the equation as a relationship, not a valuation

The SEC financial-statement guide describes the balance sheet as a snapshot. Book equity is the accounting residual; it is not a promise of the price a business could sell for. A resource can be useful without all of its economic value appearing as a recognized asset. The equation alone does not tell you whether customer demand, liquidity or future profitability is strong.

Owner investment and earned income both can increase equity but represent different activity. Borrowing increases an obligation rather than creating revenue. An owner withdrawal reduces the owner’s interest; it is not automatically an operating expense. This simplified service example uses an accrual view with no tax, inventory, depreciation, interest or other omitted period adjustments.

Buying the equipment exchanges $5,000 cash for $5,000 equipment: total assets do not change in this simplified initial-recognition example. Earning the service creates $4,000 receivable and increases equity through income. Collecting $2,500 exchanges receivable for cash without earning the revenue again. Incurring the unpaid $1,200 expense increases liabilities and reduces equity; paying $700 later reduces cash and the liability together.

The last row balances: $26,500 = $6,500 + $20,000. An equal balance is necessary, but it does not prove that the correct accounts, period or amounts were used. Recording the same wrong amount on both sides still balances.

Cash is $18,000 + $6,000 − $5,000 + $2,500 − $700 − $800 = $20,000. Equity is $18,000 investment + $4,000 earned revenue − $1,200 expense − $800 withdrawal = $20,000. Included profit is $2,800, while cash increased $20,000 from zero because investment, borrowing and asset purchases also affect cash. Do not use the cash change as profit.

Find errors the equality will not reveal

Use source documents to confirm that a customer collection has not been recorded as new revenue. Check that a bank loan was not posted to sales and that an equipment payment was not automatically treated as an immediate period expense. Review whether an unpaid invoice belongs to the period. Each error can leave a superficially balanced ledger.

A negative equity balance is possible and needs analysis of accumulated losses, distributions and the relevant accounting basis. It is not a negative cash balance by definition. Likewise, an asset-heavy business can have little available cash. See current asset classification and current liability classification before interpreting short-term coverage.

Use the equation inside a complete close

Start from reconciled opening balances, post supported transactions, complete necessary period adjustments and compare detailed accounts with the statement totals. Retain the prior version and the reason for every correction. Do not force a difference into equity just to make the statement balance.

The AR workflow and AP workflow help support two common balances. Continue with financial statement analysis for the relationship among performance, balances and cash. This primer does not replace journal-entry policy, a full closing checklist or specialist review.

Trace a real document through all four financial statements

The SEC’s financial-statement primer distinguishes a reporting-date balance sheet from statements covering a period. Use that distinction when explaining the original eight transactions: the $6,000 borrowing belongs in the liability balance and cash movement, not in sales. The $2,500 customer collection reduces an existing receivable without adding another $2,500 of earned income. The identity of the bank transaction is insufficient without its source document.

For each actual entry, retain the date, economic event, supporting document, debit, credit and reviewer. OpenStax’s transaction-analysis lesson shows how supported events affect the equation. The article’s owner-investment and withdrawal labels describe a simplified owner-equity case; a corporation would normally use its own share-capital, retained-earnings and distribution accounts. Changing the entity’s account labels does not authorize classifying a dividend as wages or a loan as revenue.

A useful final check is to explain the difference between $2,800 included profit and $20,000 closing cash without a plug. Owner investment contributes $18,000; the loan contributes $6,000; equipment acquisition uses $5,000; part of earned revenue remains in the $1,500 receivable; $500 of expense remains unpaid; and the owner withdraws $800. Each belongs in its own bridge. A proposed market sale price or an estimate of employee expertise must not be inserted into the balance sheet simply to increase equity. Recognition and measurement require the applicable accounting policy, not an attractive valuation story.

Frequently asked questions

Is equity the same as cash?

No. Equity is assets less liabilities under the accounting basis. Cash is one asset.

Does collecting an invoice create revenue again?

Not when the revenue was already recognized. It exchanges receivable for cash.

Does a loan increase profit?

Borrowed principal creates an obligation; it is not earned revenue.

Does a balanced equation prove the accounts are correct?

No. Wrong accounts, duplicated transactions or incorrect timing can still preserve equality.

Why are the closing cash balance and included profit different?

Investment, financing, asset purchases, unpaid customer and supplier balances, and withdrawals affect cash and equity differently.

Can a corporation reuse the owner-equity labels unchanged?

Use accounts appropriate to the legal entity and reporting framework. Share capital, retained earnings and distributions require their own supported records.
