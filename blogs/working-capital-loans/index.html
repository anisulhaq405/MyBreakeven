Working-Capital Loans: Size the Gap and Test Repayment

A working-capital loan funds short-term operating needs, but the label does not define one product or prove that borrowing is affordable. Identify the cash gap, the asset or receipt expected to release cash, and the date repayment is due. A line of credit can bridge a collection cycle; it cannot by itself fix a recurring contribution shortfall.

All numerical examples are original fictional USD teaching cases. They are not market rates, customer results, eligibility determinations or forecasts. Replace assumptions with dated records.

What a working-capital loan is trying to fund

Working capital describes operating resources and obligations, while working-capital financing describes a funding use. Providers can deliver it through a term loan, revolving line or qualifying asset-backed arrangement, each with different conditions. A positive current-assets-minus-current-liabilities balance does not mean enough unrestricted cash is available on next Friday’s payroll date.

Start with the working-capital balance-sheet calculation if you need that measure. This guide instead asks how much cash must bridge a specific operating cycle. Examples include paying for stock before customers pay, seasonal supplier purchases or a verified invoice collection delay. Do not treat permanent losses as temporary merely because the borrowing product has a short name.

Pre-opening needs belong in a separate startup funding schedule. When stock is involved, inventory carrying value is not automatically the cash it will realize or the amount a lender accepts. Do not assume an unawarded grant will repay a due facility.

An original four-week operating gap

A distributor starts with $4,000 unrestricted cash and wants a $3,000 minimum reserve. In week 1 it pays $9,000 for stock and operating costs; in week 2 it pays another $5,000. Customer collections of $18,000 arrive in week 3, when $2,000 operating payments also fall due. Week 4 has $3,000 further payments. Assume no other cash movements in this base schedule.

The lowest unfinanced balance is negative $10,000. To keep the stated $3,000 reserve throughout, the peak preliminary draw need is $13,000. The business finishes at the reserve without borrowing in this simplified operating case, which shows that the problem is initially timing. Interest and fees have not yet been included.

Add the proposed draw, interest and repayment

Assume a fictional line permits a $13,000 draw before the week-one payments, with a 12% nominal annual simple interest rate on the outstanding principal and a $100 draw fee paid separately. If the $13,000 stays outstanding for 28 days, interest is $13,000 × 12% × 28 ÷ 365 = $119.67 after presentation rounding. Total modeled financing expense is $219.67, not 12% of the entire facility limit.

Under those assumptions, the original $13,000 peak need must allow for the separately paid $100 fee. An initial $13,100 draw would cover that fee if the facility permits it, but it changes the interest calculation. This illustrates why financing costs must be inserted into the dated schedule rather than added after declaring the gap funded. Do not read the $13,000 as a final approved borrowing amount.

Even if the week-three collection arrives, repaying all principal plus charges can leave the week-four reserve short. The original operating schedule finishes with exactly $3,000; financing expense must come from additional operating cash, an explicitly reduced reserve or another supported adjustment. A temporary timing gap and the cost of solving it are separate requirements.

Test whether repayment comes from real collected cash

A financed inventory purchase should connect to supported sales and collections, not simply to another borrowing request. Record how stock converts to an invoice and how the invoice converts to cash. If the $18,000 collection above slips to week 5, the assumed week-three repayment is not available, and the draw may need to remain outstanding longer. A maturity or clean-down requirement can then create pressure even when the customer eventually pays.

For a separate recurring-loss case, suppose operations consume $2,000 more cash than they collect every month before debt servicing. A $12,000 advance covers six such $2,000 deficits only before fees, interest and repayments. It does not create an ongoing repayment source. Review pricing, costs and the business plan rather than repeatedly calling that pattern a one-off collection delay.

Availability can depend on more than an unused credit limit

Read the facility’s maturity, renewal, draw conditions, rate resets, minimum charges, security and guarantee provisions. For asset-backed funding, eligible receivables or inventory and concentration limits may restrict the borrowing base. An approved headline limit is not necessarily available to draw in full on every date.

The OCC describes receivables and inventory as collateral-based commercial lending resources. SBA’s 7(a) information also includes working-capital uses subject to program and lender criteria. These sources do not establish a particular business’s borrowing base or approval. Where an invoice is the main funding asset, compare the advance, reserve and recourse mechanics before making a collection assumption.

Build a repeatable funding review

  1. Build the unfinanced cash schedule and identify its low point.
  2. Choose a documented minimum reserve and identify the receipt expected to restore cash.
  3. Insert usable proceeds, draw fees, interest dates, principal payments and maturity.
  4. Stress late collection, unsold stock and reduced invoice eligibility.
  5. Check that repayment does not recreate the operating shortfall or require an unconfirmed renewal.

Use the cash-flow forecast for dated payments and the cash runway calculator for a separately defined burn assumption. Business loan comparison addresses complete offer cost. Keep the balance-sheet working-capital measure alongside, not in place of, the cash calendar.

Frequently asked questions

Is a working-capital loan always a line of credit?

No. The term describes a funding use. Term loans, lines and suitable asset-backed arrangements can have different structures and conditions.

Does positive working capital mean payroll cash is available?

No. Receivables and stock may not be cash on the payment date. Review the dated collection and payment schedule.

How is the peak preliminary funding gap calculated?

Identify the lowest unfinanced cash balance, then add the supported minimum reserve. Financing fees and repayments must be inserted before finalizing the amount.

Do I pay interest on the whole credit limit?

The contract controls. The illustration assumes simple interest on outstanding drawn principal; actual minimum fees, unused-line charges or other terms can differ.

Can a short-term loan solve a recurring operating loss?

Borrowing supplies temporary cash, but it does not remove the loss or establish repayment capacity. Rework the underlying operating assumptions.

Is an unused approved limit guaranteed to remain available?

Do not assume so. Draw conditions, collateral eligibility, maturity and renewal terms can restrict availability.
