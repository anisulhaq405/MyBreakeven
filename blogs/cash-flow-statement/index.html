Cash Flow Statement: Format, Example and Preparation

A cash flow statement explains how cash changed during a completed reporting period. It groups movements into operating, investing and financing activities, then reconciles opening to closing cash. Unlike an income statement, it follows cash movements rather than simply recognizing sales and expenses. Unlike a forecast, it reports what happened.

For the fictional Cedar Supply example, March 2026 profit is $9,000, operating cash flow is $2,000, equipment uses $12,000 and net financing provides $10,000. Total cash movement is zero, so opening and closing cash are both $10,000. The unchanged balance hides a reliance on new borrowing. All amounts are USD teaching assumptions, not customer results or industry benchmarks.

Cash flow statement format: the three sections

Classify the activity before totaling the payment
SectionQuestionExamples in a nonfinancial business
OperatingWhat did ordinary business activity collect and pay?Customer receipts; supplier and employee payments
InvestingWhat cash went into or came from long-term assets?Equipment bought for cash; cash proceeds from a sale
FinancingWhat cash came from or returned to capital providers?Loan proceeds; debt principal repaid; owner capital or distributions

Cash flow means money moving during an interval. Cash is a balance at a date. The statement links the two: closing cash = opening cash + operating cash flow + investing cash flow + financing cash flow, with foreign-exchange or other required reconciling effects separately identified when applicable. This example has none. Use one consistent cash and cash-equivalent perimeter at both dates. Transfers between included bank accounts are internal movements, not new receipts or expenses.

A complete cash flow statement example

Cedar Supply is an invented US-style nonfinancial reseller. The simplified example treats interest paid and income tax paid as operating cash flows, equipment purchases as investing, and debt principal and owner distributions as financing. It has no foreign exchange, restricted cash, cash-equivalent trades, business combinations, leases, asset sales or noncash acquisitions. Do not apply these simplified classifications to another reporting framework without checking its rules.

Operating receipts of $88,000 less $86,000 operating payments give $2,000. Then $2,000 − $12,000 + $10,000 = $0 net change. Add this to the $10,000 opening balance. Neither the $15,000 loan nor the owner distribution belongs in revenue or operating profit.

Direct versus indirect method: the same operating total

The direct method groups actual operating receipts and payments. The indirect method reconciles accrual profit to operating cash by adjusting for noncash items, operating asset and liability changes, and other items requiring reclassification. These are alternative presentations of the operating section; they do not create different closing cash balances.

Here the income statement is $100,000 revenue − $55,000 COGS − $20,000 payroll − $10,000 other operating expense − $3,000 depreciation − $1,000 interest − $2,000 tax = $9,000 net income. The indirect bridge is $9,000 + $3,000 depreciation − $12,000 receivables increase − $5,000 inventory increase − $1,000 prepayment increase + $7,000 supplier-payables increase + $1,000 payroll-accrual increase = $2,000 OCF.

Depreciation is added back because it reduced profit without being a current cash payment. It is not a cash receipt. The equipment payment is reported separately in investing. How to prepare a cash flow statement from your records

  1. Set the dates and perimeter. List included bank accounts, cash and qualifying equivalents; reconcile both endpoints to their records.
  2. Collect supporting schedules. Use the income statement, comparative balance sheets, bank and cash ledgers, receivable/payable roll-forwards, fixed-asset register, borrowing records and equity movements.
  3. Identify actual cash events. Separate accrual entries, write-offs and other noncash movements from bank payments. Do not treat every balance-sheet difference as cash.
  4. Build the operating section. Choose the presentation, classify operating balances and cross-check unusual movements. Reconcile direct and indirect views when both are available.
  5. Build investing and financing. Use gross proceeds and cash payments where required. Check loan principal separately from interest and owner distributions separately from payroll.
  6. Reconcile closing cash. Investigate differences and document excluded noncash transactions and framework-required disclosures.

A simple bank export will not identify all accrual adjustments or tell you whether a payment buys an asset. For example, a $12,000 payable increase caused by equipment acquired on credit must not be added into operating cash as though it were an unpaid inventory bill. Use transaction detail, not a universal “current liabilities” shortcut.

How to read the result without confusing cash and profit

Ask where the money came from before deciding whether a positive balance is healthy. Cedar Supply earned $9,000 but generated only $2,000 operating cash. It also spent $12,000 on equipment. New borrowing, net of repayments and distributions, kept the bank balance unchanged. Without that $10,000 net financing, modeled closing cash would be zero; the example does not establish that operations can fund the same investment repeatedly.

Negative investing cash may represent productive expansion, while positive investing cash may come from selling equipment. Positive operating cash may be temporary if overdue bills have increased. Review several comparable periods, customer collections, inventory and payment obligations. A statement alone cannot show whether a payroll payment earlier in the month was covered; it aggregates the completed period.

Frequently asked questions

What are the three parts of a cash flow statement?

Operating activities describe ordinary business cash; investing covers items such as long-term asset purchases; financing covers capital raised or returned. Their totals reconcile opening and closing cash, with other required reconciling effects identified separately.

Is a cash flow statement the same as a cash flow forecast?

No. The statement explains a completed period. A forecast places expected future receipts and payments on dates. Use verified closing actual cash as the next forecast opening balance.

Can a profitable business have low operating cash flow?

Yes. In the fictional example, net income is $9,000 but OCF is $2,000 because operating assets absorb cash, partly offset by increased operating liabilities.

Does the CSV automatically prepare a compliant statement?

No. It is a values-only teaching schedule. You must classify and verify records, calculate results and meet the reporting framework and recipient requirements separately.

Are cash-equivalent and interest classifications universal?

No. Follow the applicable framework, transaction facts and reporting period. This example states its simplified US-style nonfinancial-business assumptions; IFRS presentation requirements can differ.
