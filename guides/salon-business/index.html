Product cost is one appointment input. Rebooking describes repeat visits; no-show losses depend on costs actually avoided. The whole-business plan also needs monthly commitments and deliverable capacity.

Start with the problem in your records

Follow the steps for your decision. The examples below use fictional USD amounts; replace them with your own records.

More rebookings are not becoming more completed visits

  1. Define the repeat-visit cohort

    Keep baseline visits, the booking window and follow-up delivery period separate. Adjust for attendance and available slots.

  2. Check missed-slot economics

    Separate equivalent refills, costs avoided and costs already incurred. Gross missed revenue is not the same as contribution lost.

  3. Test the whole diary

    Bring the completed service mix into the monthly plan with committed payroll, owner pay and stylist capacity.

A membership or colour service is consuming the diary

  1. Cost the products actually mixed

    Record grams used, developer and waste. Product cost does not include every appointment expense.

  2. Cost the full appointment

    Include paid stylist time, preparation, cleanup and fees consistently; keep committed payroll separate.

  3. Reserve capacity for membership use

    Translate member-months into redeemed appointments, especially full-use and peak-time scenarios. Collections alone do not establish deliverable profit.

Gather these records before you calculate

  • Completed baseline visits, rebookings and the defined follow-up window.
  • Actual attendance, cancelled slots, equivalent refills and retained non-refundable fees.
  • Products used, avoidable commission or labour, processing fees and committed payroll.
  • Service duration, preparation/cleanup, stylist skills and spare slots in the delivery period.

Keep customer identities and private financial details out of shared copies. If a figure is missing, mark the result provisional rather than treating the missing cost as zero.

Worked check: spare slots cap the rebooking gain

The rate change creates 200 × (60% − 40%) = 40 extra bookings. Attendance reduces these to 32 expected visits, but 25 spare slots cap the model. Each completed visit leaves $80 − $25 − $2.40 = $52.60.

The seven visits beyond spare capacity are excluded. These are fictional assumptions, not a prediction about reminder software. Additional contribution still needs to help fund overhead; assess stylist skill and service duration as well as slot counts.

Connect the service menu to available appointment hours

Separate completed visits from inquiries and future bookings. Include preparation, cleanup and the staff time each service requires. A free chair is only useful when the right stylist is available for that service.

Keep variable commission or delivery labor separate from committed payroll. Count owner pay once. Retail sales, booth rent and membership collections need their own units before they join the monthly plan.
