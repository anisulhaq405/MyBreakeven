Start with the problem in your records

Follow the steps for your decision. The examples below use fictional USD amounts; replace them with your own records.

The completed job did not match the estimate

  1. Preserve the original job comparison

    Match cost categories and distinguish worker-hours, crew elapsed hours and equipment hours.

  2. Separate added scope from an overrun

    Include added and genuinely avoided costs alongside extra customer revenue and credits.

  3. Review the equipment allowance

    Use the stated operating/ownership boundary and productive machine hours. Do not count fuel or depreciation twice.

A profitable route is not funding the quiet months

  1. Check one route

    Include service and driving time, the crew cost basis and the stated overhead allocation.

  2. Make active months fund annual commitments

    Keep the full-year target alongside active-month contribution and achievable workload.

  3. Check crew capacity and cash dates

    Separate available productive worker-hours from machine hours and collection timing. Do not assume every available hour sells.

Gather these records before you calculate

  • Accepted scope, quoted revenue, change approvals and supplier credits.
  • Estimated and actual worker-hours, loaded rates and machine operating hours.
  • Materials used, travel, disposal, subcontractors and equipment-cost boundaries.
  • Annual commitments, active service months, productive crew capacity and payment dates.

Keep customer identities and private financial details out of shared copies. If a figure is missing, mark the result provisional rather than treating the missing cost as zero.

Worked check: annual commitments exceed seasonal capacity

Fictional USD example; the stated cost boundary applies
Input or resultAmount
Job price / direct cost before percentage fee$120 / $60
Payment fee / job contribution$3.60 / $56.40
Annual commitments / active service months$24,000 / 8
Required annual whole jobs426
Even monthly target / capacity54 / 50 jobs
Contribution at 400 delivered jobs / annual gap$22,560 / −$1,440

Annual break-even is $24,000 ÷ $56.40, rounded upward to 426 jobs. An even $3,000 active-month target needs 54 whole jobs; scheduling 54 in each of eight months would deliver 432, slightly above the annual minimum. Capacity of 50 per active month permits only 400.

The $1,440 gap is under these fictional assumptions. Use your actual service mix and season. Quiet months still need a dated cash plan; annual profitability cannot establish that payroll is funded on its due date.

Keep the job, route and business costs consistent

Count a worker-hour for each person working an hour. Keep that measure separate from a crew's elapsed site time and a machine's operating hours. Put fuel, maintenance and equipment ownership allowances in one place so a quote and the monthly plan do not count them twice.

For seasonal work, separate annual commitments, active-month delivery capacity and cash collection dates. A profitable job can still leave a shortfall if the business cannot deliver enough work or collect payment in time.

The guides use hypothetical worked examples. Their figures are planning assumptions, not local market rates or predicted results.
