Start with the problem in your records

Follow the steps for your decision. The examples below use fictional USD amounts; replace them with your own records.

The completed job left less than the quote

  1. Preserve the accepted estimate

    Compare the same job and cost boundary. Keep approved extra work distinct from an estimating error or service callback.

  2. Reconcile the final job

    Record first-visit actuals, then callbacks and credits once. Use actual net payment fees when a refund changes them.

  3. Use evidence in the next quote

    Revise the specific time or scope assumption for comparable work; one exceptional job does not set every future price.

The contract looks profitable but payroll is tight

  1. Check the account economics

    Use one contract period, a stated visit schedule and account costs. Check which expenses the tool includes.

  2. Budget the whole month

    Separate completed visits and variable costs from committed payroll, owner pay and overhead.

  3. Place receipts beside payroll dates

    Test when cleared customer payments arrive. A positive contract result cannot fund an earlier payroll by itself.

Gather these records before you calculate

  • Accepted scope, first-visit versus maintenance tasks and the agreed job or contract price.
  • Estimated and actual paid crew-hours, with a consistent loaded hourly cost.
  • Supplies used, travel, actual processing fees, callbacks and customer credits.
  • Completed visits, committed payroll, monthly overhead, owner pay and expected collection dates.

Keep customer identities and private financial details out of shared copies. If a figure is missing, mark the result provisional rather than treating the missing cost as zero.

Worked check: first-visit contribution is not the final result

Fictional USD example; the stated cost boundary applies
Input or resultAmount
Quoted revenue / estimated contribution$240 / $110.80
First-visit actual contribution$78.80
Callback: labour + supplies + travel$25 + $4 + $8 = $37
Customer credit / retained revenue$20 / $220
Final variable costs / contribution$198.20 / $21.80
Final contribution versus estimate−$89.00

The $32 first-visit reduction, $37 callback and $20 credit explain the $89 difference. The credit reduces revenue; it is not also entered as an expense. Actual processing fees remain $7.20 in this fictional job.

Count each cost once and review the cause before changing a quote. The $21.80 still needs to help fund monthly overhead and any costs outside these job inputs.

Choose the right cost boundary

A job quote answers what one visit must charge. A contract review looks at one account. A monthly break-even plan combines contribution with overhead, owner pay and capacity. Cash planning also needs the dates money is collected and paid.

Start with the decision you are making, and keep each cost in one place. The guides include hypothetical worked examples; their figures are not local market rates.
