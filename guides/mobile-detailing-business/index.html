Start with the problem in your records

Follow the steps for your decision. The examples below use fictional USD amounts; replace them with your own records.

A higher-ticket distant job pays less per occupied hour

  1. Compare the complete visit

    Include service, setup, travel, total distance and fees on the same owner-earnings boundary.

  2. Cost product usage consistently

    Use purchase volume, dilution and waste. Do not confuse concentrate with ready-to-use solution.

  3. Set a travel condition for the next quote

    Inspect cost and occupied time before assuming a surcharge will sell. Apply the same trip-cost basis once.

A cancellation or refill changes the planned route

  1. Rebuild the route after the cancellation

    Recalculate trips, avoided costs and equivalent replacement contribution. A rescheduled job is not automatically recovered today.

  2. Check water and refill time

    Use measured usable capacity and consumption. A refill matters financially when it adds cost or displaces deliverable work.

  3. Recheck the monthly target

    Use representative completed-job contribution and total delivery hours rather than a perfect route repeated every day.

Gather these records before you calculate

  • Agreed vehicle condition, service scope and customer access requirements.
  • Service, setup, driving, waiting and refill time for the complete route.
  • Round-trip distance, vehicle-cost basis, chemicals used and any allocated overhead.
  • Cancellation timing, saved trips/costs, replacement work and actual retained fees.

Keep customer identities and private financial details out of shared copies. If a figure is missing, mark the result provisional rather than treating the missing cost as zero.

Connect the ticket price to the complete working day

Include normal setup, pack-down and paid travel consistently. A two-person hour is two worker-hours; vehicle expense is separate from paid time in the vehicle. Put each supply, equipment and overhead allowance in one place.

A cancelled stop can change the remaining route's cost. A refill can remove an appointment from the day even when the customer price leaves positive contribution. Use the route guides for those event checks before carrying the assumptions into a monthly target.

The worked figures are hypothetical planning examples, not local market rates or predicted results. The tools use your inputs and do not guarantee bookings, route access or payment collection.
