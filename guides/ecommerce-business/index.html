Start with the problem in your records

Follow the steps for your decision. The examples below use fictional USD amounts; replace them with your own records.

Reported ROAS looks good but orders leave little money

  1. Build the complete order contribution

    Subtract product, fulfilment, shipping, fees and expected losses before testing acquisition. Use the advertised order mix.

  2. Calculate the advertising threshold

    Enter a pre-ad contribution margin. Do not include ad spend in that margin and subtract the same spend again.

  3. Check whole-store break-even

    Include acquisition once, then add overhead and owner pay. Passing the ad threshold alone does not cover the monthly business.

Returns or stock purchases are absorbing cash

  1. Define a returned-order loss

    Include refunds and return costs alongside recoverable stock. Read the calculator definition before averaging its result.

  2. Reconcile the cohort

    Track the original sales cohort, returns still open and the actual recovery cutoff. State the denominator for any per-order allowance.

  3. Place the next inventory payment

    Model replenishment dates and cash separately from COGS attributed to sold orders. Profitable stock can still need funding before it sells.

Gather these records before you calculate

  • Order revenue after discounts, with sales tax and customer-paid shipping treated consistently.
  • Landed product cost, fulfilment, packaging, outbound shipping subsidy and actual fees.
  • Return cohorts: refunds, recovered inventory value, reverse shipping, handling and retained fees.
  • Advertising spend and attributable orders for matching dates, plus inventory payment dates and monthly overhead.

Keep customer identities and private financial details out of shared copies. If a figure is missing, mark the result provisional rather than treating the missing cost as zero.

Worked check: ad break-even differs from store break-even

Fictional USD example; the stated cost boundary applies
Input or resultAmount
Selling revenue per order$50.00
Product / fulfilment and shipping / return-loss allowance$20 / $6 / $2
Payment fees: 3% of $50$1.50
Pre-ad contribution / margin$20.50 / 41%
Advertising cost per order / remaining contribution$8 / $12.50
Monthly commitments / required whole orders$2,500 / 200

Pre-ad contribution is $50 − $20 − $6 − $2 − $1.50 = $20.50. Its ad-only break-even ROAS is 1 ÷ 0.41 = approximately 2.44×, using the unrounded ratio. At $8 advertising cost per order, the remaining $12.50 funds $2,500 of monthly commitments at 200 orders.

At exactly the ad-only threshold, no contribution remains for overhead. This fictional example treats $2 as a net expected loss allowance before ads; do not add the same refund or inventory loss elsewhere.

Keep receipts, contribution and inventory cash separate

An order, an item and a paying subscriber are different units. Use the same unit for revenue, costs and capacity. Returns can reduce retained revenue and recover stock; a chargeback can leave different fees and inventory outcomes.

Include product cost, fulfillment, shipping subsidy, platform and payment fees, expected losses and acquisition once. Keep the cash spent buying inventory visible alongside the cost attributed to sold orders. A profitable order mix can still require more cash before the stock sells.

These are hypothetical planning examples, not forecasts or current platform fee schedules. Replace assumptions with your own records. Physical retail staffing and receipts require their own operating model.
