PROFILE

Business planning shaped by running a business.

Anis Ul Haq's focus is the connection between a business plan and the work required to put it into practice. Costs, pricing and sales targets matter together: a revenue goal needs a clear cost base, an achievable workload and a realistic view of customer demand.

He started Daily Outfitter in November 2025 and owns the Pakistan-based clothing business. His responsibility for planning and development keeps those questions close to everyday decisions about products, offers, customer communication and operating workflows.

WHY MYBREAKEVEN EXISTS

From costing uncertainty to a clearer target.

MyBreakeven grew out of questions in his own business: how to understand the cost of a sale, connect sales activity with a monthly goal, and make decisions when the outcome is uncertain. It turns those questions into planning models with inputs that the owner can inspect and change.

The aim is to make the reasoning visible. Owners can examine what remains after direct costs, what the business needs to cover each month, and whether the required work fits the resources available.

The Daily Outfitter founder account gives a more detailed example of that experience. It explains the original business problem, the actions he described and a costing reconstruction readers can explore.

03

WORKING APPROACH

Connect the numbers with the business decision.

MyBreakeven reflects four connected planning questions. Together, they move the discussion from a headline sales number toward the assumptions and work behind it.

  1. 01

    What does each sale contribute?

    Start with a consistent selling unit and the costs of delivering it. Look beyond the invoice value to understand the amount available for the business's wider commitments.

  2. 02

    What monthly target needs to be funded?

    Connect contribution with overhead, owner-income needs and a clearly defined profit goal. Keep those boundaries visible so the same cost is not counted twice.

  3. 03

    Can the business deliver the required work?

    Compare the target with productive time and available resources. A mathematically balanced plan can still require more work than the business can complete.

  4. 04

    What needs to be tested in practice?

    Examine demand assumptions, sales activity and operating choices. Compare scenarios, then use actual business records to review the assumptions behind the plan.
