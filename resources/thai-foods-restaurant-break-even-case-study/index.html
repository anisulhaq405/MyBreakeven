A clearer daily plan, with the calculations visible

Thai Foods, described by its manager as a five-year-old full-service restaurant in Jacksonville, Florida, wanted a clearer answer to a practical question: how many table bills must the team complete to support its desired profit? Mr. Samule, Operational Manager, manages sales and the monthly budget. According to the supplied follow-up, his earlier monthly manual process did not give him a clear target.

The corrected screenshot shows a $99 average table bill and a $50,000 additional monthly profit target. After the entered variable costs, each bill contributes $54.56. Funding overhead, owner pay and the profit goal requires 1,476 whole table bills per month. The manager translated that into a plan of about 50 table bills per day across 30 open days.

The useful outcome reported here is clearer target setting and attention to daily delivery. The monthly target is a conditional calculation, not a booking forecast. Open the restaurant break-even calculator for the tool, or follow the exact reconstruction below.

The $24,500 overhead was described as rent, electricity and FOH-related costs. The organizer clarified that direct salaries are excluded from this total and entered separately at $10.50 per bill. A detailed FOH breakdown was not supplied. Owner pay is a separate planning allowance; its treatment in statutory accounts may differ. Reconstructing the 1,476-table-bill monthly target

Payment/platform fees per bill = $99 × 6% = $5.94. Total variable cost = $21.50 materials + $10.50 labor + $3.50 packaging/commission + $3 promotion + $5.94 fees = $44.44. Contribution per bill = $99 − $44.44 = $54.56. This contribution funds the monthly commitments and profit goal; it is not profit on each individual bill.

The total monthly funding requirement is $24,500 overhead + $6,000 owner pay + $50,000 additional profit = $80,500. Required bills = $80,500 ÷ $54.56 = 1,475.439882…; round the final requirement upward to 1,476 bills. At that volume, modeled sales are $146,124, variable costs are $65,593.44 and contribution is $80,530.56. Subtracting overhead and owner pay leaves $50,030.56 under the entered assumptions, before any omitted costs and taxes.

Rounding matters: 1,475 bills leave only $49,976 after overhead and owner pay, $24 below the goal. These calculations assume stable average revenue, costs and fee rates over the operating range. They do not prove actual sales will occur.

Turning the monthly requirement into daily table-bill targets

The restaurant reports opening 30 days per month. Dividing 1,476 by 30 gives 49.2 bills per day on average. The manager chose about 50 per day as an operating target. Fifty daily bills over 30 days would produce 1,500 bills and $148,500 modeled monthly sales at a $99 average. With unchanged costs, the modeled remainder after overhead and owner pay is $51,340.

The daily revenue arithmetic is 50 × $99 = $4,950. The stated approximately $5,000 daily sales goal is a rounded operating goal, not a guaranteed minimum receipt. Actual bill sizes and the menu/channel mix vary. A monthly average permits quieter and busier days; it does not require identical sales every day.

The reported action was to set daily table-bill targets and focus on bringing in enough visitors. No quantified increase in visitors, actual bill count or conversion was supplied. Converting bills into covers needs actual party sizes, while a demand plan needs evidence of reservations, walk-ins and completed purchases. Keep these records separate from kitchen and labor capacity.

The reported November 1–15 accounts figure is a separate record

The organizer reports an accounts figure of $32,721 for November 1–15, 2026, after $12,250 allocated overhead and $3,000 owner pay, before tax. Both allocations are half their monthly planning amounts. November was still in progress when the information was collected. This is a reported after-owner-pay, before-tax result, not an independently verified statutory net-income figure or cash balance.

A comparable editorial half-month plan would use $12,250 overhead + $3,000 owner pay + $25,000 profit goal = $40,250. At the same $54.56 contribution, that needs 738 bills, $73,062 sales and a modeled $25,015.28 remainder. A hypothetical 50-bill daily plan across 15 days gives 750 bills and a modeled $25,670 remainder. Neither number reproduces the reported $32,721.

That difference remains unexplained because actual sales, table bills, fee bases, costs and accounting adjustments were not supplied. Do not reverse-engineer an actual bill count from the reported profit or project it into a full-month result. It also does not establish that the calculator caused higher earnings. A proper comparison needs the same period, realized averages, expense boundary and owner-pay treatment. What to capture at the next restaurant review

  1. Export completed table bills and realized sales for the exact period; distinguish diners from bills and separate refunds, taxes and tips.
  2. Reconcile inventory consumption, wastage and actual fees once. Keep committed payroll separate from genuinely variable labor.
  3. Save the full input screen, including utilization and conversion, alongside the dates and unit definition.
  4. Compare actual contribution with the $54.56 planning assumption before comparing profit with the target.
  5. Inspect daily and peak-service bottlenecks separately from monthly worker-hour capacity.
  6. Review payment dates in the dated cash forecast; modeled profit does not guarantee available bank cash.

Frequently asked questions

What does one order mean in the Thai Foods case study?

One order means one completed table bill, which may cover several guests. The $99 average revenue and per-order costs use that same unit. No average party size was supplied, so table bills cannot be relabelled as individual customers.

Is 1,476 table bills the restaurant’s operating break-even point?

No. It is the monthly volume required to fund $24,500 overhead, $6,000 owner pay and an additional $50,000 profit target at $54.56 contribution per bill. Overhead-only break-even is 450 whole bills; funding overhead and owner pay needs 560.

Why did the manager choose about 50 table bills per day?

The monthly 1,476-bill target averages49.2 over30 open days. A plan of 50 each day provides 1,500 monthly bills. At the entered $99 average, that is $4,950 daily sales; about $5,000 was the sales goal rather than guaranteed collections.

Did MyBreakeven independently verify the reported $32,721 profit?

No. The publisher was told this was the accounts figure for November 1–15, after $12,250 allocated overhead and $3,000 owner pay, before tax. The underlying sales and cost ledger were not supplied, so it is not an audited outcome or proof the calculator increased profit.

Was this a paid testimonial?

The outreach organizer reported no payment, discount, free paid-plan access or other benefit for the review, and reported that Mr. Samule permitted publication. Feedback came through a WhatsApp call and is paraphrased; no verbatim written testimonial or rating was supplied.

Should food wastage be added to the $21.50 cost again?

No. Food wastage was already included in the entered materials cost. A separate field would need to split that total into components or identify genuinely additional losses. Adding the same wastage twice would understate contribution.
