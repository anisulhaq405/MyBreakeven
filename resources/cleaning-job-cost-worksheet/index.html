Cleaning Job Cost Worksheet: Estimate vs Actual

Published October 10, 2026 · Updated October 14, 2026

A cleaning job can finish on time and still leave less money than the quote suggested. Use this worksheet to compare estimated costs with actual costs, then account for a callback, customer credit or extra work. The final result shows what remains to help cover monthly overhead.

Download the free cleaning job cost worksheet (.xlsx)

Includes a blank Job tab and a fictional Example tab. Use one currency throughout.

What the worksheet calculates

Compare revenue, paid crew-hours, loaded hourly cost, supplies, travel and payment fees. Enter additional callback costs separately. A customer credit reduces revenue; it is not also added as a cost. The worksheet shows final contribution, contribution as a share of retained revenue, and the change from your original estimate.

How to use it

  1. Open the Job tab. Enter your job reference and currency label.
  2. Complete both the Estimate and Actual columns. Enter zero when a cost genuinely did not occur. Leave a required input blank only when it is still unknown.
  3. Enter total paid crew-hours: two workers working three hours means six crew-hours. Use a loaded hourly cost that consistently includes the employment costs you intend to track.
  4. Use Actual for the first visit. In the additional entries section, enter callback time, supplies and travel only if they are absent from those first-visit figures.
  5. Enter any customer credit once. Keep the original charged first-visit revenue in Actual revenue; the credit is deducted in the final result.
  6. Leave the payment-fee override blank to estimate fees from first-visit revenue multiplied by the fee rate. Enter actual net fees when you know them, including any fee retained after a refund. For extra work, include its additional processing costs either in the total actual-fee override or in extra variable costs, once.
  7. Read Final job result and Contribution change by driver. Use the driver rows to identify whether labour, travel, a callback or a credit caused the difference.

Worked example: a $240 cleaning job

This is a fictional illustration, not an industry benchmark.

USD; first visit before callback and credit
ItemEstimateActual first visit
Revenue$240.00$240.00
Paid crew-hours × loaded hourly cost4 × $255 × $25
Supplies$12.00$15.00
Travel$10.00$14.00
Payment fees$7.20$7.20
Contribution before overhead$110.80$78.80

A callback adds one paid crew-hour at $25, $4 of supplies and $8 of travel: $37 in additional costs. A $20 customer credit reduces retained revenue to $220. Actual payment fees remain $7.20 in this illustration.

Final variable costs are $198.20. Final contribution is $21.80, or 9.9% of retained revenue. That is $89 less than the estimated contribution. The change is explained by $25 of extra first-visit labour, $3 of supplies, $4 of travel, the $37 callback and the $20 credit.

Contribution is not net profit

The result is retained revenue minus the variable costs you entered. It still needs to cover rent, software, insurance and other fixed overhead, plus any owner compensation or other costs not included in the inputs. Currency is a label; the workbook does not convert exchange rates.

Common questions

Do I enter a callback twice?

No. If its hours or supplies are already included in Actual, do not enter them again below.

What if my processor does not refund its fee?

Use the actual net payment-fee override. A customer credit does not automatically reduce the fee.

What if no callback or credit occurred?

Keep those adjustment inputs at zero. Leave only the optional fee override blank when you want the rate-based estimate.

What happens when retained revenue is zero?

The currency result remains useful, but the contribution percentage displays “n.a.” because dividing by zero does not produce a meaningful margin.

What if the result is negative?

The entered variable costs exceeded retained revenue. Review the cost drivers and the scope of the job before changing future quotes.

Can this set the right price for every cleaning business?

No. Use your own labour, travel, scope, overhead and local operating assumptions. The example is not a recommended hourly rate.
