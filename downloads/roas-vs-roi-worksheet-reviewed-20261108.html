Method and controls

Write both formulas and state what each includes

ROAS = attributed conversion revenue ÷ advertising spend. A 4× result means $4 attributed revenue for each $1 of media spend; it is not $4 profit. Where a platform tracks non-revenue conversion value, call the metric conversion-value ROAS rather than cash sales.

ROI = net return ÷ defined investment cost × 100%. Net return subtracts the costs included in that definition. Some teams report campaign profit divided by media/marketing investment; others divide by all included costs. Both can be interpretable, but only if the numerator and denominator are explicit and comparable.

ROAS is $12,000 ÷ $3,000 = 4×, or 400%. Total-cost ROI is $600 ÷ $11,400 = 5.26%. A separately labelled marketing-investment return is $600 ÷ ($3,000 + $1,200) = 14.29%. The different percentages do not conflict: their cost bases differ. The case excludes general company overhead, interest and tax, so $600 is not represented as company net income.

Reconcile the reported return to orders, attribution and cash

Google Ads’ ROI example includes production and advertising costs in its stated investment base. That supports the total-cost version used above; it does not make every marketing team’s differently defined return interchangeable. Keep a named numerator, denominator and measurement window beside every percentage.

For this fictional $12,000 campaign, prepare an order-level bridge from gross tracked value to $12,000 net attributed revenue. Show cancellations, credits and returns once. If the conversion tag sends profit or an assigned lead score rather than revenue, label the platform value accordingly; a 4× value-to-cost ratio then cannot be described as four sales dollars per advertising dollar.

Incrementality is a separate question. Google’s conversion-lift documentation distinguishes experiment-based additional value from attribution-based credit. As an independent teaching calculation, suppose a valid experiment estimated $4,500 incremental revenue against $3,000 media spend: incremental revenue ROAS would be 1.5×. The same ledger could still show 4× attributed ROAS because the numerators answer different questions. This invented experiment result is not a measurement of the site or a customer.

Before scaling, reconcile finance revenue, platform settings and the experiment’s uncertainty. Also check unsettled processor amounts and the expected return window. A correct reported ratio cannot replace evidence of additional demand, profitable fulfillment or enough cash to fund orders before collections arrive.
