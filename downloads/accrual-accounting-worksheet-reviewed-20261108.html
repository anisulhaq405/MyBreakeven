Updated method and evidence — November 8, 2026

This is an editable manual record, not an automatic calculator. Numerical examples are fictional. Keep your own supporting records and save or print a completed copy before closing.

Check recognition, billing and settlement against separate evidence

The OpenStax adjustment framework distinguishes accrued items from deferred items. Apply that distinction to the four case records: accepted work supports the revenue assessment; payroll evidence supports the wage accrual; the insurance contract supports the unused coverage; and the customer agreement supports the unearned advance. A bank statement establishes a receipt or payment, but does not establish that every recognition condition was satisfied.

For a real close, create one row per event with the performance or consumption date, invoice date, due date, settlement date and account reference. Explain any difference rather than forcing all five dates to match. If an invoice includes work still to be delivered, review the separate obligations before assigning the entire invoice to revenue. If an amount is disputed, identify whether the problem concerns the transaction price, the recognition conditions or collectibility; those are different questions.

The case’s $3,100 profit, $1,200 operating cash movement and $11,200 closing cash answer three defined questions. The owner’s $10,000 investment belongs in the funding bridge, not service revenue. Compare each reconciliation with the same reporting cutoff and included transactions. IRS Publication 538 is a separate federal tax-method reference; it does not turn this introductory financial-reporting case into a tax return.
