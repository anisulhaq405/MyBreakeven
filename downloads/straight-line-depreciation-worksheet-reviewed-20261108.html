Distinguish a changed estimate from an error in the original schedule

The OpenStax discussion of revised estimates illustrates allocating a remaining depreciable amount over a revised remaining life. That is a different task from correcting an arithmetic error. Keep the original approved schedule and document why new information changes an estimate; do not simply overwrite its life or residual assumptions without an effective date.

In the unchanged case here, April 2027 through March 2032 contains exactly sixty whole months. A genuine revision would require a separately documented carrying amount at the revision date, supported remaining life and updated residual estimate. Use those facts for the future schedule under the applicable policy. A mistake in the original cost, a wrongly entered start date and newly learned usage information are not automatically the same type of change.

Before using a partial-period result, verify the year, available-for-use date, convention, reporting cutoff and rounding treatment. Reconcile the first partial period and final residual, not just the annual formula. A tax convention cannot be selected solely because it creates the same first-year amount as this teaching case: IRS Publication 946 is the separate federal tax reference. Likewise, a switch to a usage method or an actual disposal requires its own decision. The manual worksheet preserves a working record but does not automatically implement any of these changes.
