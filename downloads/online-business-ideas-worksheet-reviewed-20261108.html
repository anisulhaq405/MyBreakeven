Test the rights, dependency and cash behind the offer

Before paying for a supposedly automated store or template package, identify exactly what you will own or be permitted to use: the domain, customer relationship, source files, product images, software account and supplier arrangement. Record restrictions on commercial use and resale. An attractive mockup does not establish that the seller owns the materials or that a marketplace will permit the proposed activity.

The FTC’s business-opportunity warning describes the danger of unsupported online-store earnings promises. Treat projected revenue as an assumption until supported by records that also show costs and unsuccessful outcomes. Check the seller, cancellation terms, references and any applicable disclosure obligations before buying an opportunity. A third-party service’s claim is not evidence that your own account, inventory or delivery system can achieve its result.

Use the existing original ecommerce case to separate unsold stock from lost cash. Paying $2,100 for fifty units and selling thirty leaves twenty units costing $840 in the simplified stock record. Cash has already left for all fifty, but that does not make the remaining twenty units another acquisition expense for each of the thirty sold. Investigate damage or unsellability separately. For a digital download, retain a dated version and test the file in the promised application; for the service, log revisions and unpaid selling time. The next decision is whether repeat orders remain deliverable at the recorded net economics, not whether the model can be described as passive income.
