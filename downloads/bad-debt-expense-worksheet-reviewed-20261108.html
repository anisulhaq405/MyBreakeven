Keep an allowance write-off separate from a tax-worthlessness conclusion

The OpenStax uncollectible-account illustration distinguishes estimation from a later removal of a customer balance. This guide demonstrates that mechanical distinction. It does not present its two-step reinstatement and collection sequence as the only recovery presentation permitted under every accounting policy.

For each event, identify what actually changed. The $900 provision changes the allowance estimate and included period expense. The $1,200 write-off removes a customer item and uses the existing allowance. The $300 reinstatement restores that item in the illustrated process; the subsequent $300 receipt must match bank evidence. Keep those references together so an automated receipt import cannot create a second collection, a second expense or a new sale.

An internal credit approval, a financial-reporting estimate, a customer-ledger write-off and a federal tax deduction do not share an automatic trigger. If tax treatment is needed, use the IRS bad-debt topic and the applicable business-debt guidance for the taxpayer and facts. Do not assume an unpaid invoice was previously included in taxable income, especially where a different tax accounting method is used. The numerical case intentionally makes no deductible-amount claim. Preserve the accounting trail even if legal collection activity or a tax assessment follows a different timetable.
