Method and controls

Start with one consistent unit and cost boundary

Operating profit = units × (net price − variable cost per unit) − fixed operating costs. Contribution per unit is net price less variable cost. The contribution ratio divides that contribution by price. Break-even units equal fixed costs divided by positive unit contribution. Required units for a profit target equal (fixed costs + target operating profit) divided by unit contribution.

If contribution is zero or negative, selling more of the unchanged offer does not cover positive fixed costs. Keep every amount in the same period and currency. Owner wages included in fixed costs must not be added again as a separate profit goal. Drawings and financing require separate cash treatment.

Measure the safety margin inside the relevant capacity range

The ACCA CVP explanation treats margin of safety as the excess of budgeted or actual sales over break-even sales. It should use the same price, cost assumptions and sales mix as the break-even calculation. It is a sales buffer under that model, not a cash reserve or a probability of avoiding loss.

At 250 units in the original fictional case, sales are $30,000 and break-even sales are $24,000. The margin of safety is 50 units or $6,000. Dividing $6,000 by $30,000 gives 20%. A loss of 50 sales units reaches zero operating profit under the original $48 unit contribution and $9,600 fixed-cost base. This statement assumes no simultaneous price, mix, variable-rate or fixed-cost change.

A capacity change requires a fresh calculation. The case’s additional $2,400 commitment raises fixed costs to $12,000, so the revised break-even is 250 units at $48 contribution. At 300 units, the revised result is $2,400 and the revised margin of safety is 50 units, or 16.67% of 300. The old 200-unit threshold no longer explains that committed cost structure.

Record exactly when the extra commitment becomes unavoidable. If a capacity purchase has already been agreed, returning to a lower sales volume may not restore the old fixed-cost base immediately. Show the applicable range, commitment date and exit terms beside each scenario rather than selecting whichever fixed-cost assumption produces the preferred answer.
