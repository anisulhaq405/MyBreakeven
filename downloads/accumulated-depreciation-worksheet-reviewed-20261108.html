Updated method and evidence — November 8, 2026

Keep impairment, disposal and ordinary depreciation as separate movements

The OpenStax long-term-asset discussion treats a revised estimate and a disposal as separate events. The $15,600 closing accumulated balance in this guide is an allocation-and-removal reconciliation under the stated cost-model assumptions. It is not an appraisal of the remaining assets and does not, by itself, establish their recoverable or market values.

For the disposed item, retain its asset ID, $12,000 cost, $8,400 accumulated allocation through disposal, $4,000 proceeds and any disposal costs. The $400 gain follows only because the case assumes no selling costs and no other carrying-amount adjustments. If costs or another relevant adjustment exist, reconcile them before calculating the result rather than treating gross sale proceeds as net gain. Do not remove another active asset’s accumulated balance simply because its amount makes the group total reconcile.

The closing gross-cost and accumulated schedules should agree on which asset IDs remain. A fully allocated asset can still be in use; a physical count may identify that fact without creating a new cost or rewriting its previous allocation. Conversely, an item with a positive carrying amount may have been disposed of or lost. Investigate the actual event. Where impairment or another adjustment applies under the reporting framework, show it separately; the simplified equation “cost less accumulated depreciation” is not a universal complete valuation model.
