Make the likelihood estimate and control decision auditable

A probability needs an event definition and a time unit. In this original example, interpret 15% as the assumed chance of at least one specified supplier interruption during the next quarter, with $16,000 as the modeled loss if it occurs. This is a deliberately simplified single-event model. A recurring incident count requires a frequency model instead; do not multiply a probability of at least one event by an assumed number of incidents without explaining the change.

The December 2025 NIST risk-estimation revision addresses cybersecurity risk, while the Canadian public-service register illustrates context, controls, residual exposure and ownership. Neither supplies this workshop’s probabilities. Record the evidence behind the original estimate and the assumptions behind the proposed 8% residual estimate. Until the backup supplier passes its qualification test, retain the uncontrolled scenario in the decision record.

For a fair comparison, charge the $600 control cost against the same quarter used for the $1,120 modeled exposure reduction. If the $600 is one-time setup but benefits last longer, build a multi-period comparison rather than repeating or omitting the setup arbitrarily. Also test the cost on the actual payment date: a control can improve a probability-weighted comparison while worsening this week’s cash low point. Approval should state who accepts the remaining exposure, what evidence would invalidate the estimate and which trigger starts the contingency action.
