Separate the recognized balance, debt terms and settlement plan

This original schedule is an introductory classified-balance-sheet case for a short-cycle US business. OpenStax’s current-liability lesson supports the listed common categories. Do not extend the simplified $3,000-current/$9,000-noncurrent loan split to a loan with different terms. Read contractual maturities, demand clauses, covenant compliance and relevant refinancing evidence under the applicable reporting framework before assigning the portions. A lender’s informal expectation of renewal is not the same evidence as an executed right or agreement.

Build a debt register with opening principal, new borrowings, principal repaid, closing principal, interest, next payment and classification assessment. Interest and principal answer different questions. The $250 principal payment in the original thirty-day schedule does not assert that no interest will be paid; the $9,750 total includes only the listed items. Add any actual interest and other omitted commitments to the separate cash plan without quietly changing the supported accounting balances.

For customer advances, reconcile opening liability plus new receipts less amounts earned, approved refunds and other supported movements. The $2,000 advance in this case remains a service obligation at cutoff; it is not assumed to require an immediate $2,000 cash repayment. Its fulfillment can still require future payroll or supplies, so excluding that automatic refund is not permission to exclude delivery cash. Record refund conditions and delivery evidence. The classification schedule, the expense budget and the dated cash forecast should reconcile through explained differences rather than being forced into one identical total.
