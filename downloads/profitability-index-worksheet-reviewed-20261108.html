Method and controls

State the cash-flow convention before calculating PI

PI = present value of future net project cash flows ÷ initial investment. Here the investment occurs at time zero, future amounts are net incremental project cash flows, and discounting uses end-of-year timing. NPV = present value of future net cash flows − initial investment. With these conventions and a positive initial outlay, PI = 1 + NPV ÷ initial investment.

Some sources use a different numerator or treat later investment outlays separately. Do not compare such ratios without reconciling the definitions. A zero initial investment makes this version undefined. Nonconventional cash flows and multiple later capital commitments need an explicitly rebuilt model.

Test feasible project combinations and make the PI convention explicit

OpenStax’s discounted-cash-flow explanation provides the PV-of-net-future-cash-flows divided by initial-investment convention used here. Another convention reports NPV divided by investment. Under this guide’s positive time-zero outlay setup, those figures differ by one: 1.1191 versus 0.1191 for the base case. They can preserve rankings within that setup while using different acceptance thresholds.

For a separate fictional indivisible-project decision, let a $30,000 budget fund A at $20,000 outlay and $26,000 future PV, or B and C at $15,000 outlay and $19,200 future PV each. Assume all PV amounts already use consistent risk, timing and rate assumptions; there are no dependencies or repeat opportunities. A has PI 1.30 and $6,000 NPV. B and C each have PI 1.28 and $4,200 NPV.

Ranking only by PI selects A first and leaves $10,000 that cannot buy B or C, producing $6,000 total NPV. Choosing B plus C uses the whole $30,000 budget and yields $8,400 total NPV. The arithmetic demonstrates why a ratio ranking is insufficient when projects cannot be split. It is an original constrained-choice illustration, not an observed investment recommendation.

List every feasible combination, include required companion investment and compare total NPV under the same stated assumptions. If future funding is also constrained, a single time-zero ratio may miss another bottleneck. Revisit working-capital recovery, terminal costs and discount-rate sensitivity before interpreting a rounded PI near its threshold as a robust decision.
