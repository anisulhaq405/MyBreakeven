Connect the customer promise to the operating numbers

Assume ten office customers each pay $600 a month: revenue is $6,000. Each requires eight worker-hours at an assumed job-varying loaded delivery cost of $30 per hour, so delivery labor is $2,400. Supplies are $30 per customer, or $300. Contribution after those included delivery costs is $3,300. Subtract $1,800 defined monthly overhead for an illustrative $1,500 operating result before omitted financing and tax items.

Ten customers need 80 worker-hours. If practical delivery capacity is 96 hours, the same eight-hour scope supports at most 12 whole customers, before considering route and scheduling constraints. A promise of unlimited additional service contradicts the cost and capacity blocks. Do not count the same salaried labor in both the $30 hourly rate and the $1,800 overhead.

Trace each changed promise through the connected blocks

The official Strategyzer canvas guidance treats the model as connected hypotheses that should be dated and revised as evidence changes. Put one accountable owner and version date on the record. Attach a short evidence reference to important assumptions rather than hiding every uncertainty in one general notes field.

In the original cleaning case, expanding from eight to ten worker-hours per customer changes the resources, activities and cost blocks immediately. Ten customers would require 100 hours against 96 available. The $900 result after unchanged overhead does not resolve that four-hour operational shortage. A new distant segment can also change travel, channels, the support relationship and the acceptable price. Do not revise only the revenue block while leaving the delivery system unchanged. State what actual change supplies the missing capacity and recheck its cost before committing to the promise.

This numerical example assumes the $30 loaded delivery rate varies with the included worker-hours and that its underlying labor is not also counted in overhead. A real salaried employee’s allocated rate may be useful for estimating full service cost without being an avoidable payment for one more customer. Mark that distinction before using the amount as variable-cost contribution or a cash forecast input. A completed canvas describes the proposed model; paid delivery, customer acceptance and repeat decisions test different parts of it. Archive the original and explain each changed block.
