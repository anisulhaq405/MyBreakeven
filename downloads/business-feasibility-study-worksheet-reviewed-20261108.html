Calculation and decision controls

Define the decision and the alternatives

Specify the offer, customer, delivery model, location where relevant, decision horizon and commitments under consideration. Include a smaller pilot or postponement as real alternatives. A study of “starting a business” is too broad to test supplier quotes, customer acceptance or the cash required before launch.

Decide in advance which evidence would change the answer. Separate confirmed facts from estimates and unknowns. Name the owner, date and source for important assumptions. Feasibility investigates whether the venture should proceed; the subsequent business plan develops how an accepted option will operate.

Passing one area does not pass the others. A popular product can fail a practical delivery test; a positive accounting result can still require more startup cash than is available. Obtain current specialist input for material location or activity requirements instead of copying a general statement that permits are “easy.”

State which evidence would reverse the decision

Iowa State Extension’s feasibility guidance examines whether a proposed idea can proceed using the resources and risks involved. The five connected tests in this article are its working structure, not a compulsory universal five-part standard. Its owner-income requirement is a planning recovery target, not automatically a deductible accounting expense.

Give each decision condition a source, accountable owner and review date. For the original three-hour service, a forecast of 50 jobs does not resolve the 40-job practical capacity limit. A revised two-hour scope must be demonstrated at the required quality and accepted price. Include rework, inspection and added supervision before approving the new 60-job ceiling. A supplier quote should identify its scope, expiry, payment dates and assumptions; a favorable amount from a different specification is not equivalent support. Funding must still resolve the separate $2,000 startup gap.

Compare proceed, pilot, revise and postpone on the same horizon and cost definitions. Record which commitments are difficult to reverse and the maximum exposure before the next evidence check. State the specific result that would stop further spending: missing permissions, a failed delivery test, insufficient buyer acceptance or unavailable funding can each matter. A “not yet supportable” conclusion is useful rather than a reason to manufacture a positive business plan. Preserve rejected alternatives and the evidence behind the decision.
