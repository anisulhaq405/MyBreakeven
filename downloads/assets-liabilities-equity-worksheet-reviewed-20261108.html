Trace a real document through all four financial statements

The SEC’s financial-statement primer distinguishes a reporting-date balance sheet from statements covering a period. Use that distinction when explaining the original eight transactions: the $6,000 borrowing belongs in the liability balance and cash movement, not in sales. The $2,500 customer collection reduces an existing receivable without adding another $2,500 of earned income. The identity of the bank transaction is insufficient without its source document.

For each actual entry, retain the date, economic event, supporting document, debit, credit and reviewer. OpenStax’s transaction-analysis lesson shows how supported events affect the equation. The article’s owner-investment and withdrawal labels describe a simplified owner-equity case; a corporation would normally use its own share-capital, retained-earnings and distribution accounts. Changing the entity’s account labels does not authorize classifying a dividend as wages or a loan as revenue.

A useful final check is to explain the difference between $2,800 included profit and $20,000 closing cash without a plug. Owner investment contributes $18,000; the loan contributes $6,000; equipment acquisition uses $5,000; part of earned revenue remains in the $1,500 receivable; $500 of expense remains unpaid; and the owner withdraws $800. Each belongs in its own bridge. A proposed market sale price or an estimate of employee expertise must not be inserted into the balance sheet simply to increase equity. Recognition and measurement require the applicable accounting policy, not an attractive valuation story.
