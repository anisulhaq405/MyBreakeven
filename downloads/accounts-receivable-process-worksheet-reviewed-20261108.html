Updated method and evidence — November 8, 2026

This is an editable manual record, not an automatic calculator. Numerical examples are fictional. Keep your own supporting records and save or print a completed copy before closing.

Resolve unapplied cash and report exceptions without hiding balances

Run three linked but separate records: the invoice and credit ledger, the receipt-allocation record, and the bank reconciliation. Microsoft’s receivables process documentation separates invoices, credits, customer payments, prepayments, refunds and settlement. It describes a product workflow rather than prescribing a universal accounting policy. Your control record should still explain which event occurred, who approved it and how it links to the customer balance.

For a separate original receipt example, a customer sends $1,200 against a supported $1,000 invoice. Apply $1,000 to that invoice and investigate the remaining $200 using remittance evidence. Do not increase revenue or create a second invoice merely to absorb the receipt. The balance may be an advance, an overpayment awaiting refund or another supported item; decide from the facts and accounting policy. Preserve both the gross bank receipt and its allocations so the customer account does not appear settled through an unexplained journal.

At review, list outstanding disputes, unallocated receipts, approved-but-unposted credits and promised payments with owner and next action. Reconcile those exceptions to the same cutoff as the $8,500 gross ledger. Keep any allowance assessment separate from cash collection; the OpenStax receivable examples distinguish earned sales from later settlement. A write-off or allowance change can alter a reported balance without collecting cash. Collection speed should not be improved cosmetically by changing due dates, netting unrelated parties or silently removing disputed invoices.
