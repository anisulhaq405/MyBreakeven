Separate a reporting method from a tax deduction and an asset valuation

The OpenStax method comparison supports the straight-line, usage and declining-balance mechanics used here. The choice concerns cost allocation under a reporting policy. It does not establish the asset’s resale value, a replacement-funding balance or a tax deduction. The case explicitly keeps the $14,000 acquisition cash amount the same under all three alternatives.

For a real asset, retain the purchase and installation evidence, readiness date, approved life and residual assumptions, method rationale and review history. For a usage method, reconcile the period’s production measure to the same unit definition used for total estimated output. Do not use machine-hours for one input and saleable units for the other. For declining balance, retain the residual cap and the policy on any method switch rather than allowing a formula to drive carrying amount below its intended floor.

Compare the effects using a common cutoff and cost boundary. The first-year service-case results of $7,600, $7,000 and $4,400 assume all depreciation reaches that period’s expenses. They cannot be copied unchanged into a manufacturer’s result if some allocation remains in unsold inventory. For federal tax, IRS Publication 946 addresses separate eligibility, basis, recovery-method and convention rules. A financial-statement five-year life is not proof that the same five-year tax treatment applies. Keep the reporting schedule and any tax schedule separately reconcilable.
