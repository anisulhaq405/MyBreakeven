Method and controls

Define the period and the cost boundary

Start with a customer-month, quarter or year. Use earned revenue after discounts, credits and returns for that period. Keep collections in a separate dated view: an unpaid invoice can create accounting revenue without available cash. Define whether your result includes acquisition spending, account support, general overhead and owner work.

Defined customer service subtotal = net revenue − directly traceable delivery and service costs. Then subtract assigned shared service costs to obtain the specifically defined allocated result. Label the boundary; do not call a partially costed margin company net income. A customer’s lifetime value uses a different horizon and needs retention assumptions.

Test the incremental effect before removing an account

The customer ledger above is a management allocation, not a standard contribution-margin income statement. A conventional contribution margin subtracts variable costs from sales; a traceable customer cost can be fixed, and a support allocation can remain payable after the customer leaves. The OpenStax relevant-cost framework supports comparing future amounts that actually differ between alternatives.

Extend Customer C with another explicitly fictional assumption: its $1,200 direct delivery cost disappears if the account ends, but all support salaries and general overhead remain. Removing C loses $2,000 revenue and avoids only $1,200 cost. The business result falls by $800, from $2,200 to $1,400, before any replacement work. The account’s negative $700 allocated result therefore does not mean closure saves $700.

Now test released capacity separately. If the team can sell replacement work, list its achievable revenue, additional materials, delivery hours and collection date. Deduct transition costs or contract penalties where supported. Compare that complete alternative with retaining C; do not count both a salary saving and revenue from the same retained employee without explaining the staffing arrangement.

Keep three columns in the decision record: amount assigned in the reporting ledger, cash actually avoided by the decision, and capacity released. Ask the delivery manager to confirm each assumption and reconcile the chosen action to the next month’s actual cost and customer service evidence.
