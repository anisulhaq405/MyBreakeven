Separate premises permission, tax treatment and usable capacity

Keep three decisions in different records: permission to conduct the activity at the address, the cash required to operate it, and any tax deduction. The SBA location guidance notes that zoning can apply to home businesses; the Arizona source is only an example for that jurisdiction. A permitted home activity does not automatically qualify its owner for a federal home-office deduction, and a possible deduction does not create local operating permission.

IRS Topic 509 describes qualifying business uses and the exclusive-and-regular-use tests that apply to many home-office claims, with specific exceptions such as qualifying storage and daycare. Do not infer a deduction merely from calling a room an office or allocating household rent in a planning budget. Preserve the relevant use records and review the actual tax requirements separately from this original operating case.

In the fictional capacity case, twenty jobs need forty delivery hours, leaving none of that delivery allowance unused. The sixteen hours already reserved for administration and selling must not be counted again as spare delivery capacity. Log actual booked time, completed jobs, cancellations and rework for the same month. If a job needs 2.5 hours, the sixteen-job ceiling leaves only $1,170 after the stated $350 commitments. Changing tax assumptions cannot repair that physical capacity shortfall. Revisit price, scope, scheduling or the owner-income target using evidence from completed work before accepting a fully loaded calendar.
